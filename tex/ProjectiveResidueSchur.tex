  \begin{abstract}
    The fermionic sub-programme of Cosmochrony locates the three-generation mass split in the
    \texorpdfstring{$J_3$}{J₃}-odd part of the squared projective endomorphism \texorpdfstring{$E_\Pi^2$}{EΠ²}
      restricted to the
    gauge-singlet generation triplet \texorpdfstring{$\Cgen$}{C³_gen}, parametrised by a single real number
      \texorpdfstring{$u$}{u}
    through
    \texorpdfstring{$E_\Pi^2|_{\Cgen}=\mathrm{diag}(1,\tfrac12+u,\tfrac12-u)$}{EΠ²|_{C³_gen} = diag(1, ½+u, ½−u)},
    with the even sector \texorpdfstring{$\mathrm{diag}(1,\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$}{diag(1, ½, ½)}, the algebraic value of
    \texorpdfstring{$(C_2-J_3^2)/C_2$}{(C₂−J₃²)/C₂} at \texorpdfstring{$C_2=2$}{C₂ = 2}.
    Its reading as the Born--Infeld even sector would require an identification between the
    conditional \texorpdfstring{$3\times3$}{3×3} model of O30 on
      \texorpdfstring{$\operatorname{Sym}^2(V_\rho)$}{Sym²(V_ρ)} and
    \texorpdfstring{$(E_\Pi^2)_{\mathrm{even}}$}{(EΠ²)_even}; no such identification is available, so that reading is
      not used
    here.
    This note fixes the structural status of \texorpdfstring{$u$}{u} before any explicit construction of
    \texorpdfstring{$E_\Pi$}{EΠ}.
    First, under explicit hypotheses on the projection (a chiral splitting of the spinor bundle, a contraction
    \texorpdfstring{$\PiS$}{Π_S}, a Lichnerowicz compression, and a symbol condition), the projected Dirac square has a
      universal Feshbach/Schur
      form
    \texorpdfstring{$E_\Pi=-\PiS\,D\,(1-P)\,D\,\PiS^{*}=-N^{\dagger}N$}{EΠ = −Π_S D (1−P) D Π_S* = −N†N}
    with \texorpdfstring{$N=(1-P)^{1/2}D\PiS^{*}$}{N = (1−P)^(1/2) D Π_S*} and
    \texorpdfstring{$P=\PiS^{*}\PiS$}{P = Π_S* Π_S}, negative semi-definite, zero-order under the symbol condition, and
    vanishing where \texorpdfstring{$P=1$}{P = 1}.
    Where the Clifford action on the spinor fibre is irreducible, the symbol condition forces
    \texorpdfstring{$P=1$}{P = 1} and \texorpdfstring{$E_\Pi=0$}{EΠ = 0}:
    a non-zero residue then needs a fibre on which the Clifford action is reducible.
    Second, the chiral block decomposition shows that \texorpdfstring{$u$}{u} is controlled by the
    \texorpdfstring{$\Deff^{\pm}$}{D^±}-transported, generation-projected part of the antiunitary chiral equivariance

    Third, the minimal non-injectivity \texorpdfstring{$c\leftrightarrow q-c$}{c ↔ q−c} is chirally symmetric, so
    \texorpdfstring{$u=0$}{u = 0} at the level of axioms A1--A3; a non-zero \texorpdfstring{$u$}{u} requires a chiral
    symmetry-breaking carried by the projection-locking axiom A4.
    The finite locking sector is proved \texorpdfstring{$J_\Pi$}{J_Π}-equivariant, so

    The oriented metaplectic step has the exact \texorpdfstring{$\mathfrak{{sl}}_2$}{sl₂} opening
    \texorpdfstring{$\alpha(t,s)=ts\,\frac{{r}}{{\sinh r}}=ts+O\!\left((ts)^2\right)$}{α(t,s) = ts r/sinh r = ts +

  \section{Position and purpose}
    \label{sec:position}

    The fermionic sub-programme~\cite{Beau2026q14} reads the admissible spinor bundle $\mathcal S_\Pi$ through
    a Lorentzian spin solder, [H-Spin], and a distinct weak factor, [H-Weak], neither of which is supplied by a source;
    it carries a gauge-singlet three-generation factor $\Cgen$ as a distinct supplied carrier.
    The generation mass split is carried by the projective endomorphism
    $E_\Pi\in\Gamma(\mathrm{End}(\mathcal S_\Pi))$, the zero-order spectral residue of non-injective
    projection in the spinorial sector~\cite{Beau2026q14}.
    On the supplied rank-three admissible triplet $\Cgen=\mathrm{span}(e_0,e_+,e_-)$ with Cartan
    generator
    $J_3=\mathrm{diag}(0,1,-1)$, the squared residue takes the normal form
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:normalform}
      E_\Pi^2\big|_{\Cgen}=\frac{C_2-J_3^2}{C_2}+u\,J_3+v\,R_{\mathrm{mix}},
    \end{equation}
    where $C_2=2$ is the spin-one Casimir, $R_{\mathrm{mix}}$ is the off-diagonal generation mixing, and
    $u$ is the single real coefficient governing the split.
    The CP-even/real sector imposes $v=0$.
    The identity $(C_2-J_3^2)/C_2 = \mathrm{diag}(1,\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$ at $C_2=2$ is algebraic and
    independent of~\cite{Beau2026a34}.
    Its reading as the Born--Infeld even sector would require an identification between the
    conditional $3\times3$ model of~\cite{Beau2026a34} on $\operatorname{Sym}^2(V_\rho)$ and
    $(E_\Pi^2)_{\mathrm{even}}$; no such identification is available, so that reading is not used
    here.
    The even part is therefore taken as:
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:even}
      (E_\Pi^2)_{\mathrm{even}}\big|_{\Cgen}=\frac{C_2-J_3^2}{C_2}=\mathrm{diag}\!\left(1,\tfrac12,\tfrac12\right).
    \end{equation}
    The only remaining unknown is therefore
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:udef}
      u=\frac{\big\langle \Pi_{J_\Pi\text{-odd}}(E_\Pi^2),\,J_3\big\rangle_{\HS}}{\langle J_3,J_3\rangle_{\HS}}
      =\tfrac12\big(\langle e_+|E_\Pi^2|e_+\rangle-\langle e_-|E_\Pi^2|e_-\rangle\big).
    \end{equation}
    This note records what is established about $u$ and isolates the single conceptual obstruction
    that remains, so that the explicit construction of $E_\Pi$ can proceed in a locked frame.
    The companion front observables of the angular and oriented-frontier
    notes~\cite{Beau2026aar, Beau2026q11of} are not the locus of $u$; they explain the obstructions and provide null
    controls, but $u$ lives in the zero-order residue of the projected Dirac operator.
    The numerical front controls are recorded in Appendix~\ref{sec:controls}.

  \section{The projective residue as a Schur complement}
    \label{sec:schur}

    Let $\PiS:\mathcal S\to\mathcal S_\Pi$ be the restriction of the admissible projection to the
    spinorial bundle, $\PiS^{*}$ the admissible minimal lift, and
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:dpidef}
      D_{\Pi,g,A}:=\PiS\circ D_{g,A}\circ \PiS^{*},\qquad P:=\PiS^{*}\PiS,
    \end{equation}
    following~\cite{Beau2026q14}.
    One does not assume $\PiS\PiS^{*}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal S_\Pi}$.
    The hypotheses below are used where a statement names them; none is supplied by a source.

    \begin{hypothesis}[Chiral splitting]
      \label{hyp:chiral-splitting}
      The bundles split chirally, $\mathcal S=\mathcal S_L\oplus\mathcal S_R$ and
      $\mathcal S_\Pi=\mathcal S_{\Pi,L}\oplus\mathcal S_{\Pi,R}$, the projection $\PiS$ preserves the splitting,
      and $D_{g,A}$ is chirality-odd.
      It extends to $\mathcal S$ and to $\PiS$ the chiral splitting of $\mathcal S_\Pi$ that the Lorentzian spin-solder
      hypothesis [H-Spin] of Q14 (Hypothesis~2.2) provides; no source supplies [H-Spin] or this extension.
    \end{hypothesis}

    \begin{hypothesis}[Contraction]
      \label{hyp:contraction}
      $\|\PiS\|\le1$, equivalently $P=\PiS^{*}\PiS\preceq 1$, so that $1-P\succeq0$ and $(1-P)^{1/2}$ exists.
      The operator $D_{g,A}$ is formally self-adjoint.
    \end{hypothesis}

    \begin{hypothesis}[Symbol condition]
      \label{hyp:symbol}
      On the open set considered, $D_{g,A}=\gamma^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}+B_0$ has Clifford principal symbol $\gamma(\xi)$ and
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:hsym}
        (1-P)^{1/2}\,\gamma(\xi)\,\PiS^{*}=0\qquad\text{for every covector }\xi.
      \end{equation}
      It is a condition on the eliminated sector, strictly weaker than the symbol-compatibility of Q14, which concerns
      the principal symbol of $D_{\Pi,g,A}$ itself (Remark~\ref{rem:q14-symbol}).
    \end{hypothesis}

    \begin{hypothesis}[Lichnerowicz compression]
      \label{hyp:lich}
      The effective spin--gauge Lichnerowicz operator
      $-(\nabla^{S,A})^2+\tfrac{R}{4}+\tfrac12\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu F^a_{\mu\nu}T^a$ of Q14 equals
      $\PiS D_{g,A}^2\PiS^{*}$.
      No source states this identity.
    \end{hypothesis}

    \begin{hypothesis}[Faithfulness of the chiral transport]
      \label{hyp:faithful}
      For each chirality, $\pi^{1/2}\,\Deff\,\PiS^{*}=0$ implies $\pi=0$, where $\pi$ is the corresponding diagonal
      block of $1-P$ and $\Deff$ the corresponding Weyl block.
      It is used only for the converse in Proposition~\ref{prop:chiralblocks}.
    \end{hypothesis}

    \begin{proposition}[Schur form of the projective residue]
      \label{prop:schur}
      Assume Hypotheses~\ref{hyp:contraction} and~\ref{hyp:lich}.
      Let $\EPi$ be the projective endomorphism of~\cite{Beau2026q14}, the remainder between the projected Dirac square
      and the effective spin--gauge Lichnerowicz operator, so that $\EPi=D^2_{\Pi,g,A}-\PiS D_{g,A}^2\PiS^{*}$.
      Then
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:schur}
        \EPi=-\PiS\,D_{g,A}\,(1-P)\,D_{g,A}\,\PiS^{*}=-N^{\dagger}N,
        \qquad N:=(1-P)^{1/2}\,D_{g,A}\,\PiS^{*}.
      \end{equation}
      In particular $\EPi\preceq0$ as a quadratic form, and $\EPi=0$ where $1-P=0$, i.e.\ in the injective limit.
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}
      The identity $D^2_{\Pi,g,A}=\PiS D_{g,A}\PiS^{*}\PiS D_{g,A}\PiS^{*}=\PiS D_{g,A}\,P\,D_{g,A}\PiS^{*}$ holds for
      any $\PiS$, so no idempotence of $P$ is used.
      Subtracting $\PiS D_{g,A}^2\PiS^{*}$, which is the Lichnerowicz operator by Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:lich}, gives the
      first form of~\eqref{eq:schur}.
      By Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:contraction}, $1-P$ is self-adjoint and non-negative, hence
      $1-P=(1-P)^{1/2}(1-P)^{1/2}$, and $N^{\dagger}=\PiS D_{g,A}(1-P)^{1/2}$ because $D_{g,A}$ is self-adjoint (the
      identity is read as a quadratic form, $(1-P)^{1/2}$ being taken weakly differentiable).
      Therefore $\PiS D_{g,A}(1-P)D_{g,A}\PiS^{*}=N^{\dagger}N\succeq0$, whence $\EPi=-N^{\dagger}N\preceq0$.
      If $1-P=0$ then $N=0$.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{lemma}[Order of the residue]
      \label{lem:order}
      Assume Hypotheses~\ref{hyp:contraction} and~\ref{hyp:symbol}.
      Then $N$ is an operator of order zero, and $\EPi=-N^{\dagger}N$ is a zero-order endomorphism.
      Conversely, if the second-order symbol of $\PiS D_{g,A}(1-P)D_{g,A}\PiS^{*}$ vanishes under
      Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:contraction}, then~\eqref{eq:hsym} holds.
    \end{lemma}

    \begin{proof}
      Write $N=a^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}+b$ with $a^{\mu}=(1-P)^{1/2}\gamma^{\mu}\PiS^{*}$ and $b$ of order zero, the
      covariant derivative being absorbed in $b$ and in the connection terms.
      The order-one coefficient is $a(\xi)=(1-P)^{1/2}\gamma(\xi)\PiS^{*}$, which vanishes by~\eqref{eq:hsym}, so
      $N=b$ and $N^{\dagger}N=b^{\dagger}b$ has order zero.
      Conversely, the second-order symbol of $N^{\dagger}N$ at $\xi$ is $a(\xi)^{\dagger}a(\xi)\succeq0$, which
      vanishes if and only if $a(\xi)=0$.
      The vanishing of the second-order symbol alone leaves, a priori, a first-order part; here the factorisation
      forces it to vanish as well, since every first-order term of $N^{\dagger}N$ contains a factor $a$ or
      a derivative of $a$.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{remark}[Idempotence of $P$]
      \label{rem:idempotence}
      $P^2=P$ holds if and only if $\PiS$ is a partial isometry, $\PiS\PiS^{*}\PiS=\PiS$.
      Indeed $P^2-P=\PiS^{*}X$ with $X=\PiS\PiS^{*}\PiS-\PiS$, the range of $X$ lies in $\mathrm{Im}\,\PiS$ and
      $\ker\PiS^{*}=(\mathrm{Im}\,\PiS)^{\perp}$, so $P^2=P$ if and only if $X=0$.
      If moreover $\PiS$ maps onto $\mathcal S_\Pi$, this is equivalent to $\PiS\PiS^{*}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal S_\Pi}$,
      the injectively resolved limit.
      The Schur form~\eqref{eq:schur} does not use it; for a projector $1-P$, $N=(1-P)D_{g,A}\PiS^{*}$.
    \end{remark}

    \begin{proposition}[Irreducible fibres carry no residue under the symbol condition]
      \label{prop:irreducible}
      Assume Hypotheses~\ref{hyp:contraction} and~\ref{hyp:symbol} on an open set, and suppose that at each point
      the Clifford algebra generated by the $\gamma(\xi)$ acts irreducibly on the fibre of $\mathcal S$, with
      $\PiS^{*}\neq0$ and a non-null covector available.
      Then $P=1$ and $\EPi=0$ on that set.
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}
      Let $K=\ker(1-P)$ and $R=\mathrm{Im}\,\PiS^{*}$.
      If $v\in K$ then $v=\PiS^{*}\PiS v\in R$, so $K\subset R$.
      By~\eqref{eq:hsym}, $\gamma(\xi)R\subset K\subset R$ for every $\xi$, so $R$ is invariant under the Clifford
      algebra; irreducibility and $R\neq0$ give $R=\mathcal S$.
      For a non-null $\xi$, $\gamma(\xi)^2=g^{-1}(\xi,\xi)\neq0$ makes $\gamma(\xi)$ invertible, so
      $\gamma(\xi)R=\mathcal S\subset K$, that is $P=1$.
      Then $(1-P)^{1/2}=0$ and $N=0$ on the set, with all its derivative terms, because the factor
      $(1-P)^{1/2}$ stands to the left of every term of $N$.
    \end{proof}

    Proposition~\ref{prop:schur} makes precise the statement of~\cite{Beau2026q14} that $E_\Pi$ is induced by the
    non-injective projection itself: under Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:contraction} it is the Schur complement of the
    eliminated sector $1-P$.
    A non-zero residue under Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:symbol} therefore requires a fibre on which the Clifford algebra
    acts reducibly, that is a multiplicity space.
    The surviving terms of $N$ are then those containing $\nabla\PiS^{*}$ and $B_0\PiS^{*}$; whether they are
    non-zero is a differential question that this note does not control.
    The algebraic obstruction is checked exactly in a finite model (Appendix~\ref{sec:symbolcheck}).

    \begin{remark}[Relation to the symbol-compatibility of Q14]
      \label{rem:q14-symbol}
      Under Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:symbol}, $(1-P)\gamma(\xi)\PiS^{*}=0$, so the principal symbol of
      $D^2_{\Pi,g,A}=\PiS D_{g,A}P D_{g,A}\PiS^{*}$ is $g^{-1}(\xi,\xi)\,\PiS\PiS^{*}$.
      The Laplace-type form with the standard Clifford principal symbol, which is the symbol-compatibility of Q14 and
      the form used in Section~\ref{sec:conventions}, holds if and only if $\PiS\PiS^{*}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal S_\Pi}$.
      In that case $\PiS$ is a coisometry and $P$ a projector, so the hypotheses of this note together with the
      symbol-compatibility of Q14 select the idempotent case of Remark~\ref{rem:idempotence}.
      Sections~\ref{sec:schur} and~\ref{sec:chiral} use only the weaker hypotheses they name.
    \end{remark}

  \section{Chiral block reduction and the localisation of $u$}
    \label{sec:chiral}

    Under the chiral splitting of Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:chiral-splitting}, the chirality operator
    $\gamma_5=i\gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^2\gamma^3$ of the effective Clifford algebra is a global section.
    No value of the effective co-metric is used.
    The Dirac operator is chirality-odd, hence off-diagonal in the chiral splitting
    $\mathcal S_\Pi=\mathcal S_L\oplus\mathcal S_R$:
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:blocks}
      D_{g,A}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\Deff^{-}\\ \Deff^{+}&0\end{pmatrix},
      \qquad
      1-P=\begin{pmatrix}\pi_{LL}&\pi_{LR}\\ \pi_{RL}&\pi_{RR}\end{pmatrix},
    \end{equation}
    with $\pi_{LL},\pi_{RR}$ self-adjoint and $\pi_{RL}=\pi_{LR}^{\dagger}$.

    \begin{proposition}[Chiral diagonal blocks of the residue]
      \label{prop:chiralblocks}
      Assume Hypotheses~\ref{hyp:chiral-splitting} and~\ref{hyp:contraction}, with the conventions of~\eqref{eq:blocks}.
      Then
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:diag}
        E_{LL}=-\PiS\,\Deff^{-}\pi_{RR}\Deff^{+}\,\PiS^{*},\qquad
        E_{RR}=-\PiS\,\Deff^{+}\pi_{LL}\Deff^{-}\,\PiS^{*}.
      \end{equation}
      If $\pi_{LL}=0$ the left-admissibility condition $P_R\EPi P_R=0$ of~\cite{Beau2026q14} holds: the non-injective
      projection eliminates no purely left-handed direction.
      Under Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:faithful} the converse holds, so that the condition is equivalent to $\pi_{LL}=0$.
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}
      Direct multiplication of~\eqref{eq:blocks} gives
      \begin{equation*}
        D_{g,A}(1-P)D_{g,A}=
        \begin{pmatrix}\Deff^{-}\pi_{RR}\Deff^{+}&\Deff^{-}\pi_{RL}\Deff^{-}\\
          \Deff^{+}\pi_{LR}\Deff^{+}&\Deff^{+}\pi_{LL}\Deff^{-}\end{pmatrix}.
      \end{equation*}
      Conjugating by $\PiS,\PiS^{*}$ and using $\EPi=-\PiS D_{g,A}(1-P)D_{g,A}\PiS^{*}$
      gives~\eqref{eq:diag}.
      The right-right block of $\EPi$ is $E_{RR}=-\PiS\Deff^{+}\pi_{LL}\Deff^{-}\PiS^{*}=-N_R^{\dagger}N_R$ with
      $N_R=\pi_{LL}^{1/2}\Deff^{-}\PiS^{*}$, because $\pi_{LL}\succeq0$ by Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:contraction}.
      If $\pi_{LL}=0$ then $N_R=0$ and $E_{RR}=0$.
      Conversely $E_{RR}=0$ gives $N_R=0$, hence $\pi_{LL}=0$ under Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:faithful}.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{corollary}[The left-admissible residue is chiral block-diagonal]
      \label{cor:block-diagonal}
      Assume Hypotheses~\ref{hyp:chiral-splitting} and~\ref{hyp:contraction}.
      On the left-admissible branch $\pi_{LL}=0$, the projective residue $\EPi$ is chiral block-diagonal,
      $E_{LR}=E_{RL}=0$, so $[\EPi,\gamma_5]=0$.
    \end{corollary}

    \begin{proof}
      Since $1-P\succeq0$ by Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:contraction}, and a positive-semidefinite
      self-adjoint operator with a vanishing diagonal block has vanishing off-diagonal blocks; hence
      $\pi_{LL}=0$ forces $\pi_{LR}=\pi_{RL}=0$.
      By~\eqref{eq:blocks} the off-diagonal blocks of $\EPi$ are then $-\PiS\Deff^{-}\pi_{RL}\Deff^{-}\PiS^{*}=0$
      and its adjoint, so $\EPi=\mathrm{diag}(E_{LL},0)$ and commutes with $\gamma_5$.
      Equivalently, this follows from $\EPi=-N^{\dagger}N\preceq0$ together with $E_{RR}=0$:
      a negative-semidefinite self-adjoint operator with a vanishing diagonal block has vanishing off-diagonal
      blocks.
      The chiral asymmetry of the residue is therefore carried by the diagonal imbalance
      $E_{LL}$ versus $E_{RR}=0$, not by a chiral commutator.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{corollary}[Localisation of $u$]
      \label{cor:localise}
      After restriction to $\Cgen$, the $J_3$-odd part of $E_\Pi^2$ is controlled by the chiral asymmetry
      of the eliminated block,
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:uasym}
        u\ \propto\ \pi_{RR}-\pi_{LL},
      \end{equation}
      dressed by the Weyl transport $\Deff^{\pm}$.
      The split $u$ is therefore, at the structural level, an intrinsic spectral asymmetry of the chiral part of the
      projective residue, not a shell average of any front cocycle.
      The proportionality~\eqref{eq:uasym} is a structural reading; the rigorous statement is
      Lemma~\ref{lem:chiral-obstruction}.
    \end{corollary}

    This raw block expression is refined in Section~\ref{sec:spinorial-locking}, where the antiunitary
    nature of $J_\Pi$ replaces the naive block difference by the transported defect $\Delta_\chi(P)$.

  \section{Stratification: $u$ vanishes unless A4 breaks chiral symmetry}
    \label{sec:strat}

    The candidate minimal non-injectivity is the parity involution $c\leftrightarrow q-c$ on
    conjugate Weil blocks, a structurally motivated hypothesis: O18 establishes Born--Infeld
    parity as a covariance of the response family and states the fibre identification as an open
    problem~\cite{Beau2026a22}.
    Its spinorial lift is the antilinear Born--Infeld parity $J_\Pi$, which exchanges the chiral
    subbundles, $J_\Pi P_L=P_R J_\Pi$~\cite{Beau2026q14}.
    A parity that exchanges $\mathcal S_L\leftrightarrow\mathcal S_R$ treats the two chiralities
    symmetrically, so the minimal eliminated block is chirally symmetric,
    $\pi_{LL}=\pi_{RR}$.

    \begin{proposition}[Chiral-symmetry stratification of $u$]
      \label{prop:strat}
      If $\pi_{LL}=\pi_{RR}$ then $u=0$.
      The conjugate pair $\{V^{(c)},V^{(q-c)}\}$ carries no preferred orientation at the level of axioms
      A1--A3, consistently with the non-premature-selection axiom A3~\cite{Beau2026m}.
      Hence at the A1--A3 level $u=0$.
      A non-zero $u$ requires $\pi_{LL}\neq\pi_{RR}$, a chiral symmetry-breaking that can originate only in
      the projection-locking axiom A4.
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}
      By Corollary~\ref{cor:localise}, $u\propto\pi_{RR}-\pi_{LL}$, so $\pi_{LL}=\pi_{RR}$ forces $u=0$.
      The minimal non-injectivity is the chirally symmetric parity $c\leftrightarrow q-c$, and A3 forbids
      selection among open directions before locking, so no chiral orientation is fixed by A1--A3.
      The only axiom that selects a resolved direction is A4, ``resolution occurs only when continuous
      Born--Infeld saturation meets the discrete shell support''~\cite{Beau2026m}; any chiral asymmetry
      $\pi_{LL}\neq\pi_{RR}$ must therefore arise there.
    \end{proof}

    The left-admissibility branch $\pi_{LL}=0$~\cite{Beau2026q14} is thus the maximal chiral asymmetry
    compatible with A4 locking, and the sign of $u$ is exchanged under $P_L\leftrightarrow P_R$.
    The even sector~\eqref{eq:even} is algebraic; reading it as the chirally symmetric part of a
    Born--Infeld saturation would require the identification declared unavailable above, and is not
    used here.
    The parameter $u$ is the chirally asymmetric part of the locking.

  \section{Spinorial locking and the $J_\Pi$-equivariance defect}
    \label{sec:spinorial-locking}

    This section refines Proposition~\ref{prop:strat}: the chirally symmetric branch is the transported
    equivalence $\pi_{LL}=\tau\,\overline{\pi_{RR}}\,\tau^{-1}$, not the naive block equality
    $\pi_{LL}=\pi_{RR}$, the bar being the complex conjugation carried by the antiunitary Born--Infeld
    lift $J_\Pi$.

    Let $V=\mathrm{span}(v_+,v_-)$ denote the fundamental spinorial doublet, with Cartan generator
    $Hv_\pm=\pm v_\pm$.
    The spinorial Born--Infeld lift of~\cite{Beau2026q14} is the antiunitary structure
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:jpidef}
      J_\Pi=\epsilon\circ\mathrm{conj},\qquad \epsilon=\sigma_z\sigma_x=i\sigma_y,
    \end{equation}
    so that, in the standard convention, $J_\Pi v_+=-v_-$ and $J_\Pi v_-=v_+$.
    Thus $J_\Pi^2=-1$ on $V$: this is the pseudoreal, or quaternionic, structure of the
    fundamental spinorial sector.

    On the symmetric square $\mathrm{Sym}^2(V)=\mathrm{span}(e_0,e_+,e_-)$ with $e_0=\sqrt{2}\,v_+v_-$,
    $e_+=v_+^2$, $e_-=v_-^2$, the induced antiunitary $J_\Pi^{(2)}$ acts by
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:jpisym}
      J_\Pi^{(2)}e_0=-e_0,\qquad J_\Pi^{(2)}e_+=e_-,\qquad J_\Pi^{(2)}e_-=e_+.
    \end{equation}
    Consequently $(J_\Pi^{(2)})^2=+1$ on $\mathrm{Sym}^2(V)$: the generation triplet carries a real
    involution, although the underlying doublet is pseudoreal.
    With $J_3=\mathrm{diag}(0,1,-1)$ on $(e_0,e_+,e_-)$, the formulas~\eqref{eq:jpisym} give
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:jpij3}
      J_\Pi^{(2)}J_3(J_\Pi^{(2)})^{-1}=-J_3,
    \end{equation}
    so the Born--Infeld spinorial conjugation exchanges the two outer generation weights.

    We now apply this to the Schur residue.
    Let $\tau:\mathcal S_R\to\mathcal S_L$ be the unitary intertwiner realising the chirality exchange
    $J_\Pi P_L=P_R J_\Pi$ of~\cite{Beau2026q14} in the chiral frame.
    Equivariance of the locking operator, $[P,J_\Pi]=0$, then forces on the diagonal chiral blocks of
    $1-P$ not the naive equality but the transported, complex-conjugated equivalence
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:transported}
      \pi_{LL}=\tau\,\overline{\pi_{RR}}\,\tau^{-1}.
    \end{equation}
    The relevant obstruction is consequently the antiunitary defect
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:defect}
      \Delta_\chi(P):=\pi_{LL}-\tau\,\overline{\pi_{RR}}\,\tau^{-1},
    \end{equation}
    which reduces to $\pi_{LL}-\tau\pi_{RR}\tau^{-1}$ on the real structure fixed by $J_\Pi^{(2)}$ on the
    generation triplet.

    \begin{lemma}[Chiral obstruction of spinorial locking]
      \label{lem:chiral-obstruction}
      Assume the projective Dirac transport is $J_\Pi$-compatible, $J_\Pi D_{g,A}J_\Pi^{-1}=\pm D_{g,A}$ and
      $J_\Pi\PiS J_\Pi^{-1}=\PiS$, as in the spinorial Born--Infeld lift of~\cite{Beau2026q14}.
      If the spinorial locking operator is $J_\Pi$-equivariant, $[P,J_\Pi]=0$, then the Schur residue
      $\EPi=-\PiS D_{g,A}(1-P)D_{g,A}\PiS^{*}$ satisfies $J_\Pi\EPi^2 J_\Pi^{-1}=\EPi^2$.
      Consequently $\langle e_+|\EPi^2|e_+\rangle=\langle e_-|\EPi^2|e_-\rangle$, and the split coefficient
      $u$ of~\eqref{eq:udef} vanishes.
      Hence a non-zero generation split requires a failure of $J_\Pi$-equivariance at projection locking,
      \begin{equation*}
        u\neq0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad [P,J_\Pi]\neq0.
      \end{equation*}
    \end{lemma}

    \begin{proof}
      Since $J_\Pi^{(2)}e_+=e_-$ and~\eqref{eq:jpij3} holds, the two outer generation weights are conjugate
      under the Born--Infeld spinorial involution.
      The assumptions $J_\Pi D_{g,A}J_\Pi^{-1}=\pm D_{g,A}$, $J_\Pi\PiS J_\Pi^{-1}=\PiS$ and $[P,J_\Pi]=0$
      imply $J_\Pi\EPi J_\Pi^{-1}=\pm\EPi$, up to the irrelevant sign induced by the chirality-odd Dirac
      transport.
      Squaring removes this sign and gives $J_\Pi\EPi^2 J_\Pi^{-1}=\EPi^2$.
      Because $J_\Pi$ is antiunitary,
      $\langle J_\Pi\psi|\EPi^2|J_\Pi\psi\rangle=\overline{\langle\psi|\EPi^2|\psi\rangle}$, and $\EPi^2$ is
      self-adjoint, so this expectation value is real.
      Taking $\psi=e_+$ and using $J_\Pi e_+=e_-$ gives
      $\langle e_-|\EPi^2|e_-\rangle=\langle e_+|\EPi^2|e_+\rangle$.
      The definition~\eqref{eq:udef} of $u$ then gives $u=0$, and the final implication is the
      contrapositive.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{remark}[Necessary condition, not a sufficient one]
      \label{rem:necessary}
      The condition $[P,J_\Pi]\neq0$ is necessary for $u\neq0$, but not sufficient.
      The commutator may be supported outside the generation triplet, or in components that do not survive
      the Dirac transport and the final projection to $\Cgen$.
      The magnitude of $u$ is therefore not determined by the abstract commutator itself: it is set by the
      $\Deff^{\pm}$-transported, generation-projected part of the antiunitary defect $\Delta_\chi(P)$
      of~\eqref{eq:defect}.
      Determining this transported and projected component is the remaining A4-level deliverable.
    \end{remark}

  \section{Locked Seeley--DeWitt conventions: operator $u$ equals spectral $u$}
    \label{sec:conventions}

    The projected square is Laplace-type, $D^2_{\Pi,g,A}=-(\nabla^{S,A})^2+E$, with
    $E=\tfrac{R}{4}+\tfrac12\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu F^a_{\mu\nu}T^a+\EPi$~\cite{Beau2026q14}, and the
    $\gamma_5$-weighted Seeley--DeWitt coefficient $a_4=(4\pi)^{-2}\tr(\tfrac12E^2-\tfrac16 RE+\dots)$ in
    the convention of~\cite{Beau2026q14} consistent with the heat-kernel manual.
    The bosonic synthesis fixes the spectral normalisation: $a_2$ is the horizontal gravitational
    response and $a_4$ the vertical gauge response of the spectral entropy functional, with induced
    couplings carrying a common factor at each Seeley--DeWitt order~\cite{Beau2026q13, Beau2026q12}.

    Three facts make the operator-level $u$ of~\eqref{eq:udef} coincide with the spectral coefficient
    that enters the effective amplitude.
    First, $u$ is quadratic in $\EPi$, so it is immune to the linear sign convention of $E$.
    Second, on the gauge-singlet $\Cgen$ the gauge curvature term vanishes, and on a flat background all curvature
    cross-terms vanish, in particular $-\tfrac16 R\EPi=0$; only $\tfrac12\EPi^2$ survives, so no linear-in-$\EPi$ term
    can separate the operator and spectral readings.
    Flatness, $R=0$, is a hypothesis of this section, taken in an effective co-metric of Lorentzian signature with
    constant coefficients; the sources leave both the coefficients and the signature of a full-rank co-metric
    conditional~\cite{Beau2026q11}.
    Third, the common $a_4$ normalisation factor cancels in the generation ratio
    $(\tfrac12+u)/(\tfrac12-u)$, so $u$ is independent of the spectral normalisation while the absolute
    mass scale carries it.
    A curvature correction $-\tfrac16 R\EPi$ would reappear only on a curved background, as a
    gravitational correction to masses, and does not affect the structural $u$.

  \section{Chirality is not a finite-fibre label}

  \section{Finite locking is $J_\Pi$-equivariant: separation of the finite and Lorentzian sectors}
    \label{sec:finite-equivariance}

    Before any Lorentzian computation, the finite sector must be excluded as a source of $u$.
    This section proves that the finite projection locking is $J_\Pi$-equivariant, so that $u$ cannot
    originate in the finite cascade and the generation split is forced to be a Lorentzian datum.

    Let $\mathrm{Lock}^{\mathrm{fin}}_S$ denote the projection-locking data of axiom A4~\cite{Beau2026m} at
    finite $q$: the continuous Born--Infeld saturation condition intersected with the discrete shell
    support on $\Heis_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$.
    Write $P_{\mathrm{fin}}=\PiS^{*}\PiS$ for the resulting finite admissible projector.

    \begin{proposition}[Finite equivariance of projection locking]
      \label{prop:finite-equivariance}
      For $q$ an odd prime, the finite locking data is invariant under the spinorial Born--Infeld parity
      $J_\Pi$, and the finite admissible configuration space carries no $J_\Pi$-odd modulus.
      Consequently
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:finite-equiv}
        [\mathrm{Lock}^{\mathrm{fin}}_S,J_\Pi]=0,\qquad [P_{\mathrm{fin}},J_\Pi]=0,
      \end{equation}
      and by Lemma~\ref{lem:chiral-obstruction} the finite generation split vanishes, $u_{\mathrm{fin}}=0$.
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}
      Both constituents of A4 are $J_\Pi$-invariant at finite $q$.
      The continuous saturation condition is Born--Infeld parity itself, whose spinorial lift is
      $J_\Pi$~\cite{Beau2026q14}, hence $J_\Pi$-invariant by construction.
      The discrete shell support is a union of orbits of the parity
      $c\leftrightarrow q-c$~\cite{Beau2026fe}; for $q$ an odd prime this involution acts as $c\mapsto -c$
      on the central character, whose only fixed point is $c=0$, so no non-trivial shell is parity-fixed
      and the support singles out no orientation.
      The locking data is therefore $J_\Pi$-invariant.
      Equivariance of $P_{\mathrm{fin}}$ then follows from the absence of a $J_\Pi$-odd deformation: a
      cascade edge $g\to gs$ is a Heisenberg translation, not a symplectic element, so it carries no
      canonical metaplectic lift, and the chiral eigendirections are complex combinations created only by
      Lorentzian complexification~\cite{Beau2026q14}.
      There is thus no finite direction along which a $J_\Pi$-conjugate pair of locked branches could split.
      The finite locked branch is therefore $J_\Pi$-equivariant, giving~\eqref{eq:finite-equiv}.
      The vanishing $u_{\mathrm{fin}}=0$ is then Lemma~\ref{lem:chiral-obstruction}.
    \end{proof}

    The numerical front controls of Appendix~\ref{sec:controls} confirm~\eqref{eq:finite-equiv} directly,
    though they do not prove it: the proof is the absence of a finite odd modulus above.
    The symmetric front average vanishes identically, $\Theta_{\mathrm{raw}}=0$ for $q\in\{61,101,151\}$,
    and the oriented frontier is re-paired by the residual automorphism $\varphi=R_b\circ J_\Pi$, the finite
    avatar of the parity.
    These zeros are not failures to detect $u$; they are the measured signature of finite
    $J_\Pi$-equivariance.

    \begin{corollary}[Finite/Lorentzian separation]
      \label{cor:separation}
      The finite A4 locking cannot generate $u$.
      A non-zero generation split can arise only in the Lorentzian completion of the Born--Infeld
      saturation, where the chiral-odd modulus first exists.
    \end{corollary}

    This separates the remaining problem cleanly.
    Let $s$ parametrise the $J_\Pi$-odd deformation of the locked block, opened only by Lorentzian
    complexification, with $J_\Pi:s\mapsto -s$, so that $\Delta_\chi(P)$ and hence $u$ are odd in $s$.
    Since $J_\Pi$ is a symmetry of the Lorentzian saturation functional $\mathcal B$, one has
    $\mathcal B(s)=\mathcal B(-s)$, so $s=0$ is automatically stationary and the locking character is fixed
    by the chiral curvature
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:muchi}
      \mu_\chi^2:=\partial_s^2\mathcal B(0).
    \end{equation}
    Interpreting A4 as selection of saturated admissible minima of $\mathcal B$,
    \begin{equation*}
      \mu_\chi^2>0\ \Rightarrow\ J_\Pi\text{-equivariant locking, }u=0;\qquad
      \mu_\chi^2<0\ \Rightarrow\ \text{a }J_\Pi\text{-conjugate saturated pair }\pm s_*,\ u\neq0,
    \end{equation*}
    and the marginal case $\mu_\chi^2=0$ is decided by the leading non-vanishing even coefficient
    $\lambda_{2k}$ of $\mathcal B(s)=\mathcal B(0)+\lambda_{2k}s^{2k}+O(s^{2k+2})$: positive stabilises the
    symmetric point ($u=0$), negative drives a higher-order spontaneous break ($u\neq0$), and vanishing to
    all accessible orders leaves $u$ an unfixed modulus, signalling that A4 alone does not determine the
    split.
    This marginal branch is a degenerate case of the second variation, not an independent source, so the
    decisive step is the necessarily Lorentzian computation of $\mu_\chi^2$, which the A4 companion~\cite{Beau2026bim}
    reduces to the Lorentzian genus of a chiral two-form; the construction of that two-form from the locking data and
    its genus are open there.

    This does not exclude a chiral frontier normalisation after Lorentzian complexification.
    It excludes only a symmetric finite-front observable.
    Once $\gamma_5$ exists, a $J_\Pi$-odd frontier cocycle may supply the discrete factor entering the A4
    matching~\cite{Beau2026q11of}, while the existence and stability of the split remain governed by the
    Lorentzian saturation curvature $\mu_\chi^2$.
    This discrete factor is identified in~\cite{Beau2026cho}: the chiral weight is the Weyl representation type,
    and in the inherited Heisenberg central-phase lift the frontier normalisation is fixed maximally; the split
    $u\neq0$ nonetheless still requires the Schur transversality below, on the same chiral object.

  \section{Schur transversality of the A4 locking defect}
    \label{sec:schur-transversality}

    Remark~\ref{rem:necessary} left the A4-level deliverable as a necessary condition that is not yet a
    criterion: the commutator $[P,J_\Pi]\neq0$ may be supported outside the generation triplet, or may die
    under the Weyl transport and the projection to $\Cgen$.
    Section~\ref{sec:finite-equivariance} reduces the modulus to the Lorentzian deformation $s$
    with saturation curvature $\mu_\chi^2$~\eqref{eq:muchi}.
    We now turn the necessary condition into an exhaustive dichotomy on the transported,
    generation-projected defect.

    With $s$ the Lorentzian $J_\Pi$-odd deformation parameter of Section~\ref{sec:finite-equivariance}, consider the
    family $\EPi(s):=-\PiS D_{g,A}(1-P(s))D_{g,A}\PiS^{*}$ with $0\preceq P(s)\preceq1$ and $P(0)=\PiS^{*}\PiS$.
    Here $D_{g,A}$ and $\PiS$ are held fixed in the formula, and $P(s)$ is not required to equal $\PiS^{*}\PiS$ for
    $s\neq0$; the first-order motion of the embedding contributes further terms~\cite{Beau2026ebj}.
    Using $\partial_s[-(1-P)]=\partial_sP$, the first-order chiral polarisation is
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:polarisation}
      \mathcal P:=\partial_s\EPi\big|_0=\PiS\,D_{g,A}\,(\partial_sP|_0)\,D_{g,A}\,\PiS^{*}.
    \end{equation}
    The positive sign is the derivative of $-(1-P)$, not a convention.
    Write $T(X):=\PiS D_{g,A}XD_{g,A}\PiS^{*}$ for the Schur transport of an operator $X$ on $\mathcal S$, and
    $T_{\mathrm{out}}(X)$ for its restriction to the outer generation weights $\mathrm{span}(e_+,e_-)$ of $\Cgen$;
    $T$ is the full operator, including its lower-order terms.
    The induced first-order contribution to $\EPi^2$ is the anticommutator $\{\EPi|_0,\mathcal P\}$ with the
    $J_\Pi$-even background; since $u$ is odd in $s$, its leading term is linear, so the vanishing of the $J_\Pi$-odd
    part of $\mathcal P$ on the outer weights removes the first-order opening of the split.

    \begin{lemma}[Symbol-level transport]
      \label{lem:weyl-faithful}
      Let $\xi$ be a non-null covector.
      Then $\gamma(\xi)^2=g^{-1}(\xi,\xi)\neq0$, so $\gamma(\xi)$ is invertible and the uncompressed symbol map
      $X\mapsto\gamma(\xi)X\gamma(\xi)$ is injective, whether $\xi$ is timelike or spacelike.
      If, however, Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:symbol} holds for every $s$ of the family, then
      $\PiS\gamma(\xi)\,(\partial_sP|_0)\,\gamma(\xi)\PiS^{*}=0$: the symbol-level transport of the polarisation
      vanishes, and $T(\partial_sP|_0)$ is a zero-order operator carried by the terms in $\nabla\PiS^{*}$ and
      $B_0\PiS^{*}$.
      The injectivity of the uncompressed symbol map therefore has no bearing on $T_{\mathrm{out}}$.
    \end{lemma}

    \begin{proof}
      $\gamma(\xi)^2=g^{-1}(\xi,\xi)\,\mathbb 1$ by the Clifford relation, which is non-zero for a non-null covector;
      if $\gamma(\xi)X\gamma(\xi)=0$, multiplying on both sides by $\gamma(\xi)^{-1}$ gives $X=0$.
      If~\eqref{eq:hsym} holds for every $s$ then $(1-P(s))\gamma(\xi)\PiS^{*}=0$ for every $s$, because
      $1-P=(1-P)^{1/2}(1-P)^{1/2}$.
      Differentiating at $s=0$ with $\gamma$ and $\PiS$ fixed gives $(\partial_sP|_0)\gamma(\xi)\PiS^{*}=0$, and its
      adjoint $\PiS\gamma(\xi)(\partial_sP|_0)=0$, since $\partial_sP|_0$ is self-adjoint.
      Multiplying gives the claim.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{definition}[Schur transversality]
      \label{def:schur-transversality}
      Let $\sigma:=\tfrac12\big(\partial_sP|_0-J_\Pi(\partial_sP|_0)J_\Pi^{-1}\big)$ restricted to the outer weights
      $\mathrm{span}(e_+,e_-)$ be the $J_\Pi$-odd part of the opened direction, a linear operator.
      The A4 locking defect is \emph{Schur-transverse} on the generation triplet if
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:transversality}
        T_{\mathrm{out}}(\sigma)=\Big(\PiS\,D_{g,A}\,\sigma\,D_{g,A}\,\PiS^{*}\Big)\Big|_{\mathrm{span}(e_+,e_-)}\neq 0.
      \end{equation}
      Otherwise it is \emph{Schur-null}.
      Under the $J_\Pi$-compatibility of Lemma~\ref{lem:chiral-obstruction}, $J_\Pi D_{g,A}J_\Pi^{-1}=\pm D_{g,A}$ and
      $J_\Pi\PiS J_\Pi^{-1}=\PiS$, the transport $T$ commutes with the $J_\Pi$-odd projection, so $T(\sigma)$ is the
      $J_\Pi$-odd part of the polarisation~\eqref{eq:polarisation}.
    \end{definition}

    \begin{proposition}[Schur dichotomy for the A4 locking defect]
      \label{prop:schur-dichotomy}
      With $\sigma$ as in Definition~\ref{def:schur-transversality}, exactly one of the following alternatives holds.
      \begin{enumerate}
        \item $\sigma=0$: the opened direction carries no $J_\Pi$-odd component on the outer generation weights, the
        quantity~\eqref{eq:transversality} vanishes, and the locking is Schur-null.
        \item $\sigma\neq0$ and $T_{\mathrm{out}}(\sigma)=0$ (the transported zero-mode locus): the
        quantity~\eqref{eq:transversality} vanishes and the locking is Schur-null.
        \item $\sigma\neq0$ and $T_{\mathrm{out}}(\sigma)\neq0$: the locking is Schur-transverse.
      \end{enumerate}
      In the Schur-null cases the $J_\Pi$-odd first-order Schur polarisation on the outer weights is absent, so the
      split does not open at first order.
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}
      The three cases are exhaustive and exclusive by the definition of $\sigma$ and of $T_{\mathrm{out}}$.
      In the first case $T_{\mathrm{out}}(\sigma)=0$ because $T$ is linear.
      The statement on the first-order opening follows from the anticommutator discussion above, since the
      $J_\Pi$-odd part of the polarisation on the outer weights is $T_{\mathrm{out}}(\sigma)$.
      Whether the saturation curvature is then marginal is a statement about the companion reduction, not proved
      here.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{lemma}[Exact metaplectic opening of the Schur defect]
      \label{lem:exact-schur-opening}
      Let $V\simeq\mathbb C^2$ carry the standard $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-triple
      \[
        E=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
        F=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
        H=[E,F],
      \]
      with $H=2J_3$ on the outer generation weights of $\operatorname{Sym}^2(V)=\langle e_0,e_+,e_-\rangle$, and let
      $g(t,s)=\exp(tE)\exp(sF)$ be the oriented metaplectic step of the cascade bridge~\cite{Beau2026q14}.
      Then
      \[
        g(t,s)=\begin{pmatrix}1+ts&t\\ s&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad\det g(t,s)=1,
      \]
      and, for $ts>0$, with $\cosh r=1+\tfrac{ts}{2}$, $r>0$ real,
      \[
        \log g(t,s)=\frac{r}{\sinh r}\left(tE+sF+\frac{ts}{2}H\right).
      \]
      Consequently the $J_\Pi$-odd Cartan component of the generator $\log g(t,s)$ on the generation triplet, produced
      by the $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ commutator $[E,F]=H$ (Q14, Proposition~6.5; the Heisenberg bracket $[X,Y]=Z$ does not
      appear in this computation), is
      \[
        \alpha(t,s)=ts\,\frac{r}{\sinh r}=ts+O\!\left((ts)^2\right),
      \]
      while the $R_{\mathrm{mix}}$ component vanishes identically, $\mu(t,s)\equiv0$.
      In particular $\alpha(t,s)\neq0$ for every oriented step with $ts>0$.
    \end{lemma}

    \begin{proof}
      The matrix form follows from $E^2=F^2=0$, giving
      $\exp(tE)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&t\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ and
      $\exp(sF)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\s&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.
      The element $g(t,s)$ lies in $\mathrm{SL}_2$ with $\operatorname{tr}g(t,s)=2+ts$, hence
      $\cosh r=\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}g(t,s)=1+\tfrac{ts}{2}$.
      For a hyperbolic $\mathrm{SL}_2$ element the logarithm is
      $\log g=\frac{r}{\sinh r}\big(g-\tfrac12(\operatorname{tr}g)\,\mathbb 1\big)$, and here
      $g(t,s)-\tfrac12(\operatorname{tr}g)\,\mathbb 1=tE+sF+\tfrac{ts}{2}H$.
      Since $H=2J_3$, the $J_3$ coefficient is $ts\,(r/\sinh r)$, and $r/\sinh r=1+O(ts)$ gives
      $\alpha(t,s)=ts+O((ts)^2)$.
      Lifting to $\operatorname{Sym}^2(V)$, the horizontal generators $E,F$ send the outer weights $e_\pm$ to the
      central weight $e_0$, so their restriction to $\langle e_+,e_-\rangle$ carries no $R_{\mathrm{mix}}$
      component; only the Cartan $J_3$ survives, whence $\mu(t,s)\equiv0$, the mixing direction being absent in the
      real symplectic model~\cite{Beau2026q14}.
      The factor $r/\sinh r$ is strictly positive for $r>0$, so $\alpha(t,s)\neq0$ for $ts>0$.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{hypothesis}[Transport of the opening to the locking operator, {[H-Tr]}]
      \label{hyp:transport}
      Let $P(s)$ be the locking operator along the $J_\Pi$-odd modulus $s$ of Section~\ref{sec:finite-equivariance}
      (distinct from the shear parameter $s$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:exact-schur-opening}).
      There is a real $\kappa\neq0$ such that the linear operator $\sigma$ of
      Definition~\ref{def:schur-transversality} satisfies $\sigma=\kappa\,\alpha(t,s)\,J_3$ for an oriented step
      with $ts>0$.
    \end{hypothesis}

    \begin{hypothesis}[Non-annihilation of the transport, {[H-Ker]}]
      \label{hyp:kernel}
      $T_{\mathrm{out}}(\sigma)\neq0$ for the operator $\sigma$ of Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:transport}.
      Under Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:symbol} along the family, $T_{\mathrm{out}}(\sigma)$ is carried by the zero-order terms
      in $\nabla\PiS^{*}$ and $B_0\PiS^{*}$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:weyl-faithful}).
    \end{hypothesis}

    No source relates $P(s)$ to the metaplectic step $g(t,s)$: Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:transport} is a missing element,
    not an excluded one, and Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:kernel} is a separate one.
    No identification of the central commutator $Z=[X,Y]$ with a temporal direction is used, and none is available:
    the central entry of the co-metric of Q5b~\cite{Beau2026q5b} (Theorem~5.2) is zero at rank three, it is positive in
    the conditional full-rank completion of Q8~\cite{BeauQ8} (Corollary~6.1), and it is negative in
    neither~\cite{Beau2026q11}.

    \begin{corollary}[Schur transversality under the transport hypotheses]
      \label{cor:schur-transversality-closed}
      Assume Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:transport}.
      Then $\sigma\neq0$, so the alternative $\sigma=0$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:schur-dichotomy} is excluded, and the
      locking is Schur-transverse if and only if $T_{\mathrm{out}}(\sigma)\neq0$.
      In particular it is Schur-transverse under Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:kernel}.
    \end{corollary}

    \begin{proof}
      By Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:transport} and Lemma~\ref{lem:exact-schur-opening}, $\sigma=\kappa\alpha J_3\neq0$.
      The remaining alternatives of Proposition~\ref{prop:schur-dichotomy} are distinguished by
      $T_{\mathrm{out}}(\sigma)$.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{corollary}[A4-level reduction]
      \label{cor:a4-reduction}
      The A4 residual problem is reduced to the projected Lorentzian datum
      \[
        \sigma=\tfrac12\big(\partial_sP|_0-J_\Pi(\partial_sP|_0)J_\Pi^{-1}\big)\big|_{\mathrm{span}(e_+,e_-)}.
      \]
      If the locking is Schur-transverse, the generation split is decided by the Born--Infeld genus of the
      chiral two-form supplied in~\cite{Beau2026bim}: $\mu_\chi^2>0$ gives $u=0$, and $\mu_\chi^2<0$ gives a
      split $u\neq0$ under the nondegeneracy hypothesis stated there.
      If the locking is Schur-null, the first-order polarisation is absent; in the reduction of~\cite{Beau2026bim}, for
      the supplied two-form, $\mu_\chi^2=0$ and the split is governed by the leading even coefficient $\lambda_{2k}$
      of~\eqref{eq:muchi}.
    \end{corollary}

    The dichotomy of Proposition~\ref{prop:schur-dichotomy} is proved under the hypotheses it names; which branch
    holds is open.
    Lemma~\ref{lem:exact-schur-opening} evaluates the $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ commutator of the oriented step in closed form;
    under Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:transport} it excludes the exact-zero branch, and under Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:kernel} the
    transported zero-mode branch is excluded.
    Neither hypothesis is supplied by a source, and the symbol-level transport vanishes
    (Lemma~\ref{lem:weyl-faithful}), so that Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:kernel} concerns zero-order terms.
    The Lorentzian datum $\partial_sP|_0$ is not constructed: its relation to the metaplectic step
    $g(t,s)=\exp(tE)\exp(sF)$ is Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:transport}.
    The sign of the Born--Infeld genus of~\cite{Beau2026bim} is not decided here.

  \section{Conclusion}
    \label{sec:conclusion}

    The generation split $u$ is localised with no remaining ambiguity of method: it is controlled by
    the $\Deff^{\pm}$-transported, generation-projected component of the antiunitary chiral equivariance
    defect
    \[
      \Delta_\chi(P)=\pi_{LL}-\tau\,\overline{\pi_{RR}}\,\tau^{-1}
    \]
    of the spinorial directions eliminated by the non-injective projection, read off the Schur complement
    $\EPi=-N^{\dagger}N$ and restricted to the gauge-singlet triplet $\Cgen$.
    It vanishes identically for the $J_\Pi$-equivariant minimal non-injectivity, and is non-zero only
    through a chiral symmetry-breaking carried by the projection-locking axiom A4.
    The polarisation, the Seeley--DeWitt conventions, and the operator/spectral identification are locked;
    the front observables are confirmed to be null controls rather than carriers of $u$.

    exact-zero branch under the transport hypothesis~\ref{hyp:transport}, and the transported zero-mode branch is

    stated there for a chiral two-form supplied as a hypothesis.
    This note does not decide it, and does not determine the value $|u|$, which remains fixed by the chiral-frontier
