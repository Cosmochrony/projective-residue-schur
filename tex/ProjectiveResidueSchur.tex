  \section{Position and purpose}
    \label{sec:position}

    The fermionic sub-programme~\cite{Beau2026q14} reads the admissible spinor bundle $\mathcal S_\Pi$ through
    a Lorentzian spin solder, [H-Spin], and a distinct weak factor, [H-Weak], neither of which is supplied by a source;
    it carries a gauge-singlet three-generation factor $\Cgen$ as a distinct supplied carrier.
    The generation mass split is carried by the projective endomorphism
    $E_\Pi\in\Gamma(\mathrm{End}(\mathcal S_\Pi))$, the zero-order spectral residue of non-injective
    projection in the spinorial sector~\cite{Beau2026q14}.
    On the supplied rank-three admissible triplet $\Cgen=\mathrm{span}(e_0,e_+,e_-)$ with Cartan
    generator
    $J_3=\mathrm{diag}(0,1,-1)$, the squared residue takes the normal form
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:normalform}
      E_\Pi^2\big|_{\Cgen}=\frac{C_2-J_3^2}{C_2}+u\,J_3+v\,R_{\mathrm{mix}},
    \end{equation}
    where $C_2=2$ is the spin-one Casimir, $R_{\mathrm{mix}}$ is the off-diagonal generation mixing, and
    $u$ is the single real coefficient governing the split.
    The CP-even/real sector imposes $v=0$.
    The identity $(C_2-J_3^2)/C_2 = \mathrm{diag}(1,\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$ at $C_2=2$ is algebraic and
    independent of~\cite{Beau2026a34}.
    Its reading as the Born--Infeld even sector would require an identification between the
    conditional $3\times3$ model of~\cite{Beau2026a34} on $\operatorname{Sym}^2(V_\rho)$ and
    $(E_\Pi^2)_{\mathrm{even}}$; no such identification is available, so that reading is not used
    here.
    The even part is therefore taken as:
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:even}
      (E_\Pi^2)_{\mathrm{even}}\big|_{\Cgen}=\frac{C_2-J_3^2}{C_2}=\mathrm{diag}\!\left(1,\tfrac12,\tfrac12\right).
    \end{equation}
    The only remaining unknown is therefore
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:udef}
      u=\frac{\big\langle \Pi_{J_\Pi\text{-odd}}(E_\Pi^2),\,J_3\big\rangle_{\HS}}{\langle J_3,J_3\rangle_{\HS}}
      =\tfrac12\big(\langle e_+|E_\Pi^2|e_+\rangle-\langle e_-|E_\Pi^2|e_-\rangle\big).
    \end{equation}
    In~\eqref{eq:normalform} and~\eqref{eq:udef}, $E_\Pi^2|_{\Cgen}$ stands for the compression to $\Cgen$ of the
      generation
    reading $G=\Phi(E_\Pi^2)$ of $E_\Pi^2$ on the generation space of Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:generation}; Q14 supplies no
    such reading~\cite{Beau2026q14}.
    This note records what is established about $u$ and isolates the single conceptual obstruction
    that remains, so that the explicit construction of $E_\Pi$ can proceed in a locked frame.
    The companion front observables of the angular and oriented-frontier
    notes~\cite{Beau2026aar, Beau2026q11of} are not the locus of $u$; they explain the obstructions and provide null
    controls, but $u$ lives in the residue $\EPi[P]$ of the projected Dirac operator, which is zero-order under
    the symbol condition of Section~\ref{sec:schur}.
    The numerical front controls are recorded in Appendix~\ref{sec:controls}.

    \paragraph{Scope.}
    Two frameworks are distinguished.
    For the reference operator $P_{\mathrm{ref}}=\PiS^{*}\PiS$, the compression remainder $\EPi$ and its properties
      (Schur
    form, order, irreducible-fibre obstruction) are established under the hypotheses of Section~\ref{sec:schur}, and
      under
    the generation-reading hypothesis [H-Gen] the framework gives $u=0$.
    For a general locking operator $P\neq P_{\mathrm{ref}}$, only the Schur expression $\EPi[P]$ and its sign are
    unconditional; the formula $\EPi=D^2_{\Pi,g,A}-\PiS D_{g,A}^2\PiS^{*}$, the Laplace-type form and the
    Seeley--DeWitt identification of Section~\ref{sec:conventions} do not transfer to $\EPi[P]$, and every consequence
      for
    a non-zero split is conditional on [H-Gen], on the transport hypothesis [H-Tr] and on the other named hypotheses.
    The note therefore localises necessary conditions (a locking operator different from $P_{\mathrm{ref}}$ that breaks
    $J_\Pi$-equivariance) and missing constructions (the transport hypothesis, the generation reading, a finite
    reading); it derives no physical mechanism for a non-zero split.
    The reference framework is compatible with the $J_\Pi$-odd structure of Q14 only where the
    reference residue is not left-admissible (Remark~\ref{rem:q14-left}): under the chiral splitting and [H-Gen](i), a
    non-zero left-admissible endomorphism of Q14 is not $\EPi[P_{\mathrm{ref}}]$.

  \section{The projective residue as a Schur complement}
    \label{sec:schur}

    Let $\PiS:\mathcal S\to\mathcal S_\Pi$ be the restriction of the admissible projection to the
    spinorial bundle, $\PiS^{*}$ the admissible minimal lift, and
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:dpidef}
      D_{\Pi,g,A}:=\PiS\circ D_{g,A}\circ \PiS^{*},\qquad P_{\mathrm{ref}}:=\PiS^{*}\PiS,
    \end{equation}
    following~\cite{Beau2026q14}.
    One does not assume $\PiS\PiS^{*}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal S_\Pi}$.
    A \emph{locking operator} is a self-adjoint operator $P$ on $\mathcal S$ with $0\preceq P\preceq1$.
    The reference operator $P_{\mathrm{ref}}$ is a locking operator under Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:contraction} below.
    From Section~\ref{sec:chiral} on, $P$ denotes a locking operator, and $P_{\mathrm{ref}}=\PiS^{*}\PiS$ is a
      particular
    one; every statement says which it uses.
    For a locking operator $P$ write
    $\EPi[P]:=-\PiS D_{g,A}(1-P)D_{g,A}\PiS^{*}$, and $\EPi:=\EPi[P_{\mathrm{ref}}]$.
    The hypotheses below are used where a statement names them; none is supplied by a source.

    \begin{hypothesis}[Chiral splitting]
      \label{hyp:chiral-splitting}
      The bundles split chirally, $\mathcal S=\mathcal S_L\oplus\mathcal S_R$ and
      $\mathcal S_\Pi=\mathcal S_{\Pi,L}\oplus\mathcal S_{\Pi,R}$, the projection $\PiS$ preserves the splitting,
      and $D_{g,A}$ is chirality-odd.
      It extends to $\mathcal S$ and to $\PiS$ the chiral splitting of $\mathcal S_\Pi$ that the Lorentzian spin-solder
      hypothesis [H-Spin] of Q14 (Hypothesis~2.2) provides; no source supplies [H-Spin] or this extension.
    \end{hypothesis}

    \begin{hypothesis}[Contraction]
      \label{hyp:contraction}
      $\|\PiS\|\le1$, equivalently $P_{\mathrm{ref}}\preceq 1$.
      The operator $D_{g,A}$ is formally self-adjoint for the positive Hermitian structure on the spinor bundles; the
      Clifford relation $\gamma(\xi)^2=g^{-1}(\xi,\xi)$ is read up to the sign convention of that structure.
    \end{hypothesis}

    \begin{hypothesis}[Symbol condition]
      \label{hyp:symbol}
      On the open set considered, $D_{g,A}=\gamma^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}+B_0$ has Clifford principal symbol $\gamma(\xi)$ and
      the locking operator $P$ considered satisfies
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:hsym}
        (1-P)^{1/2}\,\gamma(\xi)\,\PiS^{*}=0\qquad\text{for every covector }\xi.
      \end{equation}
      It is a condition on the eliminated sector (see Remark~\ref{rem:q14-symbol} for its relation to Q14).
    \end{hypothesis}

    \begin{hypothesis}[Faithfulness of the chiral transport]
      \label{hyp:faithful}
      For each chirality, $\pi^{1/2}\,\Deff\,\PiS^{*}=0$ implies $\pi=0$, where $\pi$ is the corresponding diagonal
      block of $1-P$ and $\Deff$ the corresponding Weyl block.
      It is used only for the converse in Proposition~\ref{prop:chiralblocks}.
    \end{hypothesis}

    \begin{proposition}[Schur form of the projective residue]
      \label{prop:schur}
      Let $P$ be a locking operator and $N_P:=(1-P)^{1/2}D_{g,A}\PiS^{*}$.
      Then
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:schur}
        \EPi[P]=-\PiS\,D_{g,A}\,(1-P)\,D_{g,A}\,\PiS^{*}=-N_P^{\dagger}N_P\preceq0
      \end{equation}
      as a quadratic form, and $\EPi[P]=0$ where $P=1$.
      Under Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:contraction}, for $P=P_{\mathrm{ref}}$, $\EPi=\EPi[P_{\mathrm{ref}}]=D^2_{\Pi,g,A}-\PiS
      D_{g,A}^2\PiS^{*}$ is the compression remainder of the projected Dirac square
      (Remark~\ref{rem:q14-symbol} relates it to the projective endomorphism of~\cite{Beau2026q14}).
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}
      Since $1-P$ is self-adjoint and non-negative, $1-P=(1-P)^{1/2}(1-P)^{1/2}$, and
      $N_P^{\dagger}=\PiS D_{g,A}(1-P)^{1/2}$ because $D_{g,A}$ is self-adjoint (the identity is read as a quadratic
      form, $(1-P)^{1/2}$ being taken weakly differentiable).
      Hence $\PiS D_{g,A}(1-P)D_{g,A}\PiS^{*}=N_P^{\dagger}N_P\succeq0$, which gives~\eqref{eq:schur}.
      For $P=P_{\mathrm{ref}}$, $D^2_{\Pi,g,A}=\PiS D_{g,A}\PiS^{*}\PiS D_{g,A}\PiS^{*}=\PiS
      D_{g,A}P_{\mathrm{ref}}D_{g,A}
      \PiS^{*}$ holds for any $\PiS$, so no idempotence is used, and subtracting $\PiS D_{g,A}^2\PiS^{*}$ gives the
      stated form.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{lemma}[Order of the residue]
      \label{lem:order}
      Let $P$ be a locking operator satisfying Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:symbol}.
      Then $N_P$ is an operator of order zero, and $\EPi[P]=-N_P^{\dagger}N_P$ is a zero-order endomorphism.
      Conversely, if the second-order symbol of $\PiS D_{g,A}(1-P)D_{g,A}\PiS^{*}$ vanishes, then~\eqref{eq:hsym} holds.
    \end{lemma}

    \begin{proof}
      Write $N_P=a^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}+b$ with $a^{\mu}=(1-P)^{1/2}\gamma^{\mu}\PiS^{*}$ and $b$ of order zero, the
      covariant derivative being absorbed in $b$ and in the connection terms.
      The order-one coefficient is $a(\xi)=(1-P)^{1/2}\gamma(\xi)\PiS^{*}$, which vanishes by~\eqref{eq:hsym}, so
      $N_P=b$ and $N_P^{\dagger}N_P=b^{\dagger}b$ has order zero.
      Conversely, the second-order symbol of $N_P^{\dagger}N_P$ at $\xi$ is $a(\xi)^{\dagger}a(\xi)\succeq0$, which
      vanishes if and only if $a(\xi)=0$.
      The vanishing of the second-order symbol alone leaves, a priori, a first-order part; here the factorisation
      forces it to vanish as well, since every first-order term of $N_P^{\dagger}N_P$ contains a factor $a$ or
      a derivative of $a$.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{remark}[Idempotence of $P_{\mathrm{ref}}$]
      \label{rem:idempotence}
      $P_{\mathrm{ref}}^2=P_{\mathrm{ref}}$ holds if and only if $\PiS$ is a partial isometry,
      $\PiS\PiS^{*}\PiS=\PiS$.
      Indeed $P_{\mathrm{ref}}^2-P_{\mathrm{ref}}=\PiS^{*}X$ with $X=\PiS\PiS^{*}\PiS-\PiS$, the range of $X$ lies in
      $\mathrm{Im}\,\PiS$ and $\ker\PiS^{*}=(\mathrm{Im}\,\PiS)^{\perp}$, so the equality holds if and only if $X=0$.
      If moreover $\PiS$ maps onto $\mathcal S_\Pi$, this is equivalent to $\PiS\PiS^{*}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal S_\Pi}$,
      the injectively resolved limit.
      The Schur form~\eqref{eq:schur} does not use it.
    \end{remark}

    \begin{proposition}[Irreducible fibres carry no residue under the symbol condition]
      \label{prop:irreducible}
      Assume Hypotheses~\ref{hyp:contraction} and~\ref{hyp:symbol} for the reference operator $P=P_{\mathrm{ref}}$ on an
      open set, and suppose that at each point the Clifford algebra generated by the $\gamma(\xi)$ acts irreducibly on
      the fibre of $\mathcal S$, with $\PiS^{*}\neq0$ and a non-null covector available.
      Then $P_{\mathrm{ref}}=1$ and $\EPi=0$ on that set.
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}
      Let $K=\ker(1-P_{\mathrm{ref}})$ and $R=\mathrm{Im}\,\PiS^{*}$.
      If $v\in K$ then $v=\PiS^{*}\PiS v\in R$, so $K\subset R$; this uses $P=P_{\mathrm{ref}}$.
      By~\eqref{eq:hsym}, $\gamma(\xi)R\subset K\subset R$ for every $\xi$, so $R$ is invariant under the Clifford
      algebra; irreducibility and $R\neq0$ give $R=\mathcal S$.
      For a non-null $\xi$, $\gamma(\xi)^2=g^{-1}(\xi,\xi)\neq0$ makes $\gamma(\xi)$ invertible, so
      $\gamma(\xi)R=\mathcal S\subset K$, that is $P_{\mathrm{ref}}=1$.
      Then $(1-P_{\mathrm{ref}})^{1/2}=0$ and $N=0$ on the set, with all its derivative terms, because the factor
      $(1-P_{\mathrm{ref}})^{1/2}$ stands to the left of every term of $N$.
    \end{proof}

    A non-zero residue $\EPi$ under Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:symbol} therefore requires a fibre on which the Clifford algebra
    acts reducibly, that is a multiplicity space.
    The surviving terms of $N$ are then those containing $\nabla\PiS^{*}$ and $B_0\PiS^{*}$; whether they are
    non-zero is a differential question that this note does not control.
    The algebraic obstruction is checked exactly in a finite model (Appendix~\ref{sec:symbolcheck}).

    \begin{remark}[Relation to the projective endomorphism of Q14]
      \label{rem:q14-symbol}
      Q14 defines its projective endomorphism by $\EPi^{\mathrm{Q14}}:=D^2_{\Pi,g,A}-\mathrm{Lich}$, where
      $\mathrm{Lich}$ is the spin--gauge Lichnerowicz operator
      $-(\nabla^{S,A})^2+\tfrac{R}{4}+\tfrac12\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu F^a_{\mu\nu}T^a$, and assumes that the principal
      symbol of $D_{\Pi,g,A}$ is the projected Clifford symbol.
      The compression remainder $\EPi$ equals $\EPi^{\mathrm{Q14}}$ if and only if
      $\PiS D_{g,A}^2\PiS^{*}=\mathrm{Lich}$, an identity that no source states.
      Comparing principal symbols, that identity forces $\PiS\PiS^{*}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal S_\Pi}$: the symbol of
      $\mathrm{Lich}$ is $g^{-1}(\xi,\xi)\,\mathbb 1$ and that of $\PiS D_{g,A}^2\PiS^{*}$ is
      $g^{-1}(\xi,\xi)\,\PiS\PiS^{*}$.
      Hence $\PiS$ is then a coisometry, $P_{\mathrm{ref}}$ a projector, and contraction is automatic; the converse is
      not claimed, since $\PiS\PiS^{*}=\mathrm{id}$ matches only the principal symbols.
      Q14 states $\EPi^{\mathrm{Q14}}=0$ when the projection is injectively resolved and proves it for $\PiS$
      injective with $\PiS\PiS^{*}=\mathrm{id}$, where the composition reduces to $D_{g,A}$ (it takes $\PiS$ unitary).
      Here $\PiS$ is a coisometry with non-trivial kernel, $P_{\mathrm{ref}}\neq1$, and $\PiS\PiS^{*}=\mathrm{id}$
      alone does not give $\EPi=0$, since $D^2_{\Pi,g,A}=\PiS D P_{\mathrm{ref}} D\PiS^{*}$; the identification
      concerns that case only, and does not extend Q14's statement for injective $\PiS$.
      The results for $\EPi$ apply to $\EPi^{\mathrm{Q14}}$ exactly under the identity
      $\PiS D_{g,A}^2\PiS^{*}=\mathrm{Lich}$; without it they concern the compression remainder only, and no statement
      is made about $\EPi^{\mathrm{Q14}}$.
      Under Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:symbol} for $P_{\mathrm{ref}}$, $(1-P_{\mathrm{ref}})\gamma(\xi)\PiS^{*}=0$ and the
      principal symbol of $D^2_{\Pi,g,A}$ is $g^{-1}(\xi,\xi)\,\PiS\PiS^{*}$ as well.
    \end{remark}

    From here on, $\EPi$ denotes the compression remainder $\EPi[P_{\mathrm{ref}}]$ and $\EPi[P]$ the Schur expression
      of
    a locking operator $P$.
    The normal form~\eqref{eq:normalform} and the coefficient $u$ are defined for $\EPi[P]$ with the operator $P$ of the
    statement; they concern the projective endomorphism of Q14 only under the identity
    $\PiS D_{g,A}^2\PiS^{*}=\mathrm{Lich}$ of Remark~\ref{rem:q14-symbol}.

  \section{Chiral block reduction and the localisation of \texorpdfstring{$u$}{u}}
    \label{sec:chiral}

    In Sections~\ref{sec:chiral} to~\ref{sec:schur-transversality}, $P$ is a locking operator and $\EPi$ stands for
    $\EPi[P]$; for $P=P_{\mathrm{ref}}$ it is the compression remainder.
    Under the chiral splitting of Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:chiral-splitting}, the chirality operator
    $\gamma_5=i\gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^2\gamma^3$ of the effective Clifford algebra is a global section.
    No value of the effective co-metric is used.
    The Dirac operator is chirality-odd, hence off-diagonal in the chiral splitting
    $\mathcal S_\Pi=\mathcal S_L\oplus\mathcal S_R$:
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:blocks}
      D_{g,A}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\Deff^{-}\\ \Deff^{+}&0\end{pmatrix},
      \qquad
      1-P=\begin{pmatrix}\pi_{LL}&\pi_{LR}\\ \pi_{RL}&\pi_{RR}\end{pmatrix},
    \end{equation}
    with $\pi_{LL},\pi_{RR}$ self-adjoint and $\pi_{RL}=\pi_{LR}^{\dagger}$.

    \begin{proposition}[Chiral diagonal blocks of the residue]
      \label{prop:chiralblocks}
      Assume Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:chiral-splitting}, with the conventions of~\eqref{eq:blocks} for a locking operator
      $P$.
      Then
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:diag}
        E_{LL}=-\PiS\,\Deff^{-}\pi_{RR}\Deff^{+}\,\PiS^{*},\qquad
        E_{RR}=-\PiS\,\Deff^{+}\pi_{LL}\Deff^{-}\,\PiS^{*}.
      \end{equation}
      If $\pi_{LL}=0$ the condition $P_R\EPi P_R=0$ holds, which is the left-admissibility condition
      of~\cite{Beau2026q14} when $\EPi$ is Q14's endomorphism: the non-injective
      projection eliminates no purely left-handed direction.
      Under Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:faithful} the converse holds, so that the condition is equivalent to $\pi_{LL}=0$.
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}
      Direct multiplication of~\eqref{eq:blocks} gives
      \begin{equation*}
        D_{g,A}(1-P)D_{g,A}=
        \begin{pmatrix}\Deff^{-}\pi_{RR}\Deff^{+}&\Deff^{-}\pi_{RL}\Deff^{-}\\
          \Deff^{+}\pi_{LR}\Deff^{+}&\Deff^{+}\pi_{LL}\Deff^{-}\end{pmatrix}.
      \end{equation*}
      Conjugating by $\PiS,\PiS^{*}$ and using $\EPi=-\PiS D_{g,A}(1-P)D_{g,A}\PiS^{*}$
      gives~\eqref{eq:diag}.
      The right-right block of $\EPi$ is $E_{RR}=-\PiS\Deff^{+}\pi_{LL}\Deff^{-}\PiS^{*}=-N_R^{\dagger}N_R$ with
      $N_R=\pi_{LL}^{1/2}\Deff^{-}\PiS^{*}$, because $\pi_{LL}\succeq0$ for a locking operator.
      If $\pi_{LL}=0$ then $N_R=0$ and $E_{RR}=0$.
      Conversely $E_{RR}=0$ gives $N_R=0$, hence $\pi_{LL}=0$ under Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:faithful}.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{corollary}[The left-admissible residue is chiral block-diagonal]
      \label{cor:block-diagonal}
      Assume Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:chiral-splitting}, and a locking operator $P$.
      On the left-admissible branch $\pi_{LL}=0$, the projective residue $\EPi$ is chiral block-diagonal,
      $E_{LR}=E_{RL}=0$, so $[\EPi,\gamma_5]=0$.
    \end{corollary}

    \begin{proof}
      Since $1-P\succeq0$ for a locking operator, and a positive-semidefinite
      self-adjoint operator with a vanishing diagonal block has vanishing off-diagonal blocks; hence
      $\pi_{LL}=0$ forces $\pi_{LR}=\pi_{RL}=0$.
      By~\eqref{eq:blocks} the off-diagonal blocks of $\EPi$ are then $-\PiS\Deff^{-}\pi_{RL}\Deff^{-}\PiS^{*}=0$
      and its adjoint, so $\EPi=\mathrm{diag}(E_{LL},0)$ and commutes with $\gamma_5$.
      Equivalently, this follows from $\EPi=-N^{\dagger}N\preceq0$ together with $E_{RR}=0$:
      a negative-semidefinite self-adjoint operator with a vanishing diagonal block has vanishing off-diagonal
      blocks.
      The chiral asymmetry of the residue is therefore carried by the diagonal imbalance
      $E_{LL}$ versus $E_{RR}=0$, not by a chiral commutator.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{corollary}[Localisation of $u$]
      \label{cor:localise}
      After restriction to $\Cgen$, the $J_3$-odd part of $E_\Pi^2$ is read, at the structural level, from the chiral
      asymmetry of the eliminated block,
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:uasym}
        u\ \propto\ \pi_{RR}-\pi_{LL},
      \end{equation}
      dressed by the Weyl transport $\Deff^{\pm}$.
      The split $u$ is therefore, at the structural level, an intrinsic spectral asymmetry of the chiral part of the
      projective residue, not a shell average of any front cocycle.
      The proportionality~\eqref{eq:uasym} is a structural reading; the rigorous statement is
      Lemma~\ref{lem:chiral-obstruction}.
    \end{corollary}

    This raw block expression is refined in Section~\ref{sec:spinorial-locking}, where the antiunitary
    nature of $J_\Pi$ replaces the naive block difference by the transported defect $\Delta_\chi(P)$.

  \section{Stratification of \texorpdfstring{$u$}{u} by \texorpdfstring{$J_\Pi$}{JΠ}-equivariance}
    \label{sec:strat}

    The candidate minimal non-injectivity is the parity involution $c\leftrightarrow q-c$ on
    conjugate Weil blocks, a structurally motivated hypothesis: O18 establishes Born--Infeld
    parity as a covariance of the response family and states the fibre identification as an open
    problem~\cite{Beau2026a22}.
    Its spinorial lift is the antilinear Born--Infeld parity $J_\Pi$, which exchanges the chiral
    subbundles, $J_\Pi P_L=P_R J_\Pi$~\cite{Beau2026q14}.
    A parity that exchanges $\mathcal S_L\leftrightarrow\mathcal S_R$ treats the two chiralities
    symmetrically, so on this reading the minimal eliminated block is $J_\Pi$-symmetric in the transported sense,
    $\pi_{LL}=\tau\,\overline{\pi_{RR}}\,\tau^{-1}$ (Section~\ref{sec:spinorial-locking}).

    \begin{proposition}[Chiral-symmetry stratification of $u$]
      \label{prop:strat}
      Under Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:generation}, if the locking operator $P$ commutes with $J_\Pi$ then $u[P]=0$
      (Lemma~\ref{lem:chiral-obstruction}).
      On the structurally motivated reading in which the minimal non-injectivity $c\leftrightarrow q-c$ is
      $J_\Pi$-symmetric and axiom A3~\cite{Beau2026m} forbids selection among open directions before locking, the
      locking operator commutes with $J_\Pi$ at the level of axioms A1--A3, so $u=0$ there.
      A non-zero $u$ requires $J_\Pi PJ_\Pi^{-1}\neq P$, which on that reading can originate only in the
      projection-locking
      axiom A4.
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}
      The first statement is Lemma~\ref{lem:chiral-obstruction}.
      The remaining statements are the reading of axioms A1--A4 stated in the proposition: A3 forbids a chiral
      orientation before locking, and the only axiom that selects a resolved direction is A4,
      ``resolution occurs only when continuous Born--Infeld saturation meets the discrete shell
      support''~\cite{Beau2026m}.
      They are interpretive, not derived from the axioms.
      The raw block reading $u\propto\pi_{RR}-\pi_{LL}$ of Corollary~\ref{cor:localise} is not used.
    \end{proof}

    The left-admissibility branch $\pi_{LL}=0$~\cite{Beau2026q14} is the branch on which no purely left-handed
    direction is eliminated.
    For a $J_\Pi$-commuting $P$ it forces $P=1$ and $\EPi[P]=0$ (Remark~\ref{rem:q14-left}), so a non-zero residue
    on that branch breaks $J_\Pi$-compatibility and is not covered by the $J_\Pi$-symmetric reading of the minimal
    block above; the relation of the left-admissible branch to A4 locking is not derived here.
    The sign of $u$ is exchanged under $P_L\leftrightarrow P_R$.
    The even sector~\eqref{eq:even} is algebraic; reading it as the chirally symmetric part of a
    Born--Infeld saturation would require the identification declared unavailable above, and is not
    used here.
    The parameter $u$ is the chirally asymmetric part of the locking.

  \section{Spinorial locking and the \texorpdfstring{$J_\Pi$}{JΠ}-equivariance defect}
    \label{sec:spinorial-locking}

    This section refines Proposition~\ref{prop:strat}: the chirally symmetric case is the transported
    equivalence $\pi_{LL}=\tau\,\overline{\pi_{RR}}\,\tau^{-1}$, not the naive block equality
    $\pi_{LL}=\pi_{RR}$, the bar being the complex conjugation carried by the antiunitary Born--Infeld
    lift $J_\Pi$.

    Let $V=\mathrm{span}(v_+,v_-)$ denote the fundamental spinorial doublet, with Cartan generator
    $Hv_\pm=\pm v_\pm$.
    The spinorial Born--Infeld lift of~\cite{Beau2026q14} is the antiunitary structure
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:jpidef}
      J_\Pi=\epsilon\circ\mathrm{conj},\qquad \epsilon=\sigma_z\sigma_x=i\sigma_y,
    \end{equation}
    so that, in the standard convention, $J_\Pi v_+=-v_-$ and $J_\Pi v_-=v_+$.
    Thus $J_\Pi^2=-1$ on $V$: this is the pseudoreal, or quaternionic, structure of the
    fundamental spinorial sector.

    On the symmetric square $\mathrm{Sym}^2(V)=\mathrm{span}(e_0,e_+,e_-)$ with $e_0=\sqrt{2}\,v_+v_-$,
    $e_+=v_+^2$, $e_-=v_-^2$, the induced antiunitary $J_\Pi^{(2)}$ acts by
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:jpisym}
      J_\Pi^{(2)}e_0=-e_0,\qquad J_\Pi^{(2)}e_+=e_-,\qquad J_\Pi^{(2)}e_-=e_+.
    \end{equation}
    Consequently $(J_\Pi^{(2)})^2=+1$ on $\mathrm{Sym}^2(V)$: the generation triplet carries a real
    involution, although the underlying doublet is pseudoreal.
    With $J_3=\mathrm{diag}(0,1,-1)$ on $(e_0,e_+,e_-)$, the formulas~\eqref{eq:jpisym} give
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:jpij3}
      J_\Pi^{(2)}J_3(J_\Pi^{(2)})^{-1}=-J_3,
    \end{equation}
    so the Born--Infeld spinorial conjugation exchanges the two outer generation weights.

    In what follows $V$ is the fundamental spinorial doublet of the model and $\mathcal H=\mathrm{Sym}^2(V)$ with
    $J=J_\Pi^{(2)}$ is the generation space of Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:generation}.
    Q14 keeps the generation carrier $V_{\mathrm{gen}}$ distinct from the soldered doublet $V$, for which
      $\mathrm{Sym}^2(V)$
    is the Lorentz sector, with a different internal parity~\cite{Beau2026q14}; taking $\mathcal H=\mathrm{Sym}^2(V)$
      is a
    model choice of this note, not a result of Q14.

    We now apply this to the Schur residue.
    Let $\tau:\mathcal S_R\to\mathcal S_L$ be the unitary intertwiner realising the chirality exchange
    $J_\Pi P_L=P_R J_\Pi$ of~\cite{Beau2026q14} in the chiral frame.
    Equivariance of the locking operator, $[P,J_\Pi]=0$, then forces on the diagonal chiral blocks of
    $1-P$ not the naive equality but the transported, complex-conjugated equivalence
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:transported}
      \pi_{LL}=\tau\,\overline{\pi_{RR}}\,\tau^{-1}.
    \end{equation}
    The relevant obstruction is consequently the antiunitary defect
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:defect}
      \Delta_\chi(P):=\pi_{LL}-\tau\,\overline{\pi_{RR}}\,\tau^{-1},
    \end{equation}
    which reduces to $\pi_{LL}-\tau\pi_{RR}\tau^{-1}$ on the real structure fixed by $J_\Pi^{(2)}$ on the
    generation triplet.

    \begin{hypothesis}[Generation reading and compatibility, {[H-Gen]}]
      \label{hyp:generation}
      (i) $J_\Pi$ is an antiunitary operator on $\mathcal S$ and on $\mathcal S_\Pi$ (with $J_\Pi^2=-1$ on $\mathcal
      S_\Pi$, Q14 Theorem~3.7), compatible with the Dirac transport and the projection:
      $J_\Pi D_{g,A}J_\Pi^{-1}=\pm D_{g,A}$ and $J_\Pi\PiS J_\Pi^{-1}=\PiS$.
      (ii) $\mathcal H$ is a Hilbert space with an antiunitary $J$ and an orthonormal triple $e_0,e_+,e_-$ spanning
      $\Cgen$, with $Je_0=-e_0$ and $Je_\pm=e_\mp$, so that $J^2=+1$ on $\Cgen$; in the model
      $\mathcal H=\mathrm{Sym}^2(V)$ and $J=J_\Pi^{(2)}$ of~\eqref{eq:jpisym}.
      (iii) A real-linear reading map $\Phi:\mathrm{End}(\mathcal S_\Pi)\to\mathrm{End}(\mathcal H)$ satisfies
      $\Phi(X^{*})=\Phi(X)^{*}$ and $\Phi(J_\Pi XJ_\Pi^{-1})=J\,\Phi(X)\,J^{-1}$.
      The generation operator of a locking operator $P$ is $G[P]:=\Phi(\EPi[P]^2)$, and $u[P]$ is given
      by~\eqref{eq:udef} with $G[P]$ in place of $E_\Pi^2$.
      Q14 keeps the generation carrier distinct from the soldered doublet, uses a different operator on it, and supplies
      neither this reading map nor this compatibility~\cite{Beau2026q14}; this hypothesis supplies them.
    \end{hypothesis}

    \begin{hypothesis}[Multiplicative reading, {[H-Mult]}]
      \label{hyp:mult}
      In addition to Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:generation}, the reading map is multiplicative on the algebra generated by
      the endomorphisms $\EPi[P]$ under consideration and positivity-preserving there: $\Phi(XY)=\Phi(X)\Phi(Y)$ and $\Phi(X)\succeq0$ for $X\succeq0$.
      Multiplicativity is required on the algebra generated by the whole family $\EPi[P(s)]$ and its jets, not on a
      single $\EPi[P]$.
    \end{hypothesis}

    Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:mult} is a separate condition, not part of the meaning of [H-Gen]: [H-Gen] supplies only a
    real-linear $\Phi$ commuting with $*$ and with the conjugation by $J_\Pi$, and every result of this note, including
    Lemma~\ref{lem:chiral-obstruction}, uses no more.
    Under [H-Mult], $G[P]=\Phi(\EPi[P])^{2}$ is positive, which the reading of $\tfrac12\pm u$ as eigenvalues needs, and $\EPi[P]\preceq0$ gives $\Phi(\EPi[P])\preceq0$, which fixes the sign of
    the even sector $\Phi(\EPi)$ and licenses the jet formula $u'(0)=\langle J_3,\{E_0,E_1\}\rangle/\langle J_3,J_3\rangle$
    with $E_i=\Phi(\partial_s^i\EPi)/i!$ used in the consumers of this note.

    \begin{lemma}[Chiral obstruction of spinorial locking]
      \label{lem:chiral-obstruction}
      Assume Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:generation}, and let $P$ be a locking operator, $0\preceq P\preceq1$.
      If $J_\Pi PJ_\Pi^{-1}=P$, then $J_\Pi\,\EPi[P]\,J_\Pi^{-1}=\EPi[P]$, $J\,G[P]\,J^{-1}=G[P]$, and
      $\langle e_+|G[P]|e_+\rangle=\langle e_-|G[P]|e_-\rangle$, so that $u[P]=0$.
      Hence a non-zero generation split requires a locking operator that does not commute with $J_\Pi$,
      \begin{equation*}
        u[P]\neq0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad J_\Pi PJ_\Pi^{-1}\neq P.
      \end{equation*}
      For the reference operator $P=\PiS^{*}\PiS$ the commutation holds automatically under
      Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:generation}(i); for that fixed $\PiS$ a non-zero $u$ therefore requires a locking operator
      different from $\PiS^{*}\PiS$.
    \end{lemma}

    \begin{proof}
      The assumptions give
      $J_\Pi\EPi[P]J_\Pi^{-1}=-\PiS(J_\Pi D_{g,A}J_\Pi^{-1})(1-P)(J_\Pi D_{g,A}J_\Pi^{-1})\PiS^{*}=\EPi[P]$, the two
      signs
      $\pm$ multiplying to $+1$ and $J_\Pi\PiS^{*}J_\Pi^{-1}=\PiS^{*}$ following from the antiunitarity of $J_\Pi$.
      Hence $J_\Pi\EPi[P]^2J_\Pi^{-1}=\EPi[P]^2$, and by~(iii) $JG[P]J^{-1}=G[P]$; $G[P]$ is self-adjoint because
      $\EPi[P]^2$ is and $\Phi$ preserves adjoints.
      Because $J$ is antiunitary, $\langle J\psi|G|J\psi\rangle=\overline{\langle\psi|G|\psi\rangle}$, which is real
      since $G$ is self-adjoint.
      Taking $\psi=e_+$ and using $Je_+=e_-$ gives $\langle e_-|G|e_-\rangle=\langle e_+|G|e_+\rangle$.
      The definition~\eqref{eq:udef} then gives $u[P]=0$.
      For $P=\PiS^{*}\PiS$, $J_\Pi PJ_\Pi^{-1}=(J_\Pi\PiS J_\Pi^{-1})^{*}(J_\Pi\PiS J_\Pi^{-1})=P$.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{remark}[Necessary condition, not a sufficient one]
      \label{rem:necessary}
      The condition $[P,J_\Pi]\neq0$ is necessary for $u\neq0$, but not sufficient.
      The commutator may be supported outside the generation triplet, or in components that do not survive
      the Dirac transport and the final projection to $\Cgen$.
      The magnitude of $u$ is therefore not determined by the abstract commutator itself.
      Whether it is set by the $\Deff^{\pm}$-transported, generation-projected part of the antiunitary defect
      $\Delta_\chi(P)$ of~\eqref{eq:defect} is a structural reading, not a theorem; determining this transported and
      projected component is the remaining A4-level deliverable.
    \end{remark}

    \begin{remark}[Compatibility with the left-admissible branch of Q14]
      \label{rem:q14-left}
      Q14 shows that on the left-admissible branch $P_R\,\EPi^{\mathrm{Q14}}P_R=0$ and that, if
      $\EPi^{\mathrm{Q14}}\neq0$ and $\EPi^{\mathrm{Q14}}\preceq0$, then $\EPi^{\mathrm{Q14}}$ is not
      $J_\Pi$-invariant~\cite{Beau2026q14}.
      Assume Hypotheses~\ref{hyp:chiral-splitting} and~\ref{hyp:generation}(i) and the identity of
      Remark~\ref{rem:q14-symbol}, so that $\EPi=\EPi[P_{\mathrm{ref}}]$ is the endomorphism of Q14 and satisfies
      $J_\Pi\EPi J_\Pi^{-1}=\EPi$.
      Then $\EPi\preceq0$ and $P_R\EPi P_R=0$ give $\EPi P_R=0$, hence $\EPi=P_L\EPi P_L$; the invariance and
      $J_\Pi P_L=P_R J_\Pi$ give $P_L\EPi P_L=J_\Pi^{-1}(P_R\EPi P_R)J_\Pi=0$, so $\EPi=0$.
      The same argument applies to any locking operator $P$ with $J_\Pi PJ_\Pi^{-1}=P$: then
      $\EPi[P]=-\PiS D(1-P)D\PiS^{*}$ is $J_\Pi$-invariant by Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:generation}(i), $\EPi[P]\preceq0$, and
      $P_R\EPi[P]P_R=0$ gives $\EPi[P]=0$ as above (no use of $\pi_{LL}$ is needed; by
      Proposition~\ref{prop:chiralblocks}, $\pi_{LL}=0$ implies $P_R\EPi P_R=0$).
      Under Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:chiral-splitting}, a non-zero residue on the left-admissible branch therefore requires $J_\Pi PJ_\Pi^{-1}\neq P$, or a
      $J_\Pi$ that does not preserve $\PiS$ or $D$ as in Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:generation}(i); it cannot be the
      residue of a $J_\Pi$-compatible $P_{\mathrm{ref}}$, and Q14's anomaly computation, which uses
      $\EPi=P_L\EPi P_L$, concerns a residue that this framework does not produce.
      The vanishing $u[P_{\mathrm{ref}}]=0$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:chiral-obstruction} concerns the fixed $\PiS$ of
      Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:generation}(i); it bounds neither the $J_\Pi$-odd part of Q14's endomorphism nor the split
      of a family whose embedding moves.
      In the covariant family of~\cite{Beau2026ebj} one has $J_\Pi\PiS(s)J_\Pi^{-1}=\PiS(-s)$, each
      $P(s)=\PiS(s)^{*}\PiS(s)$ is the reference operator of its own $\PiS(s)$, and the rate $u'(0)$ is non-zero.
      Its even base block is $J_\Pi$-compatible, so by the argument above it is not left-admissible unless
      $1-P(0)=0$; the statement is made for the chiral frame of that note and not for its finite witness on
      $\mathbb C^3_{\mathrm{gen}}$, whose target carries no chirality.
    \end{remark}

  \section{Seeley--DeWitt conventions for the reference residue}
    \label{sec:conventions}

    This section concerns the reference operator $P_{\mathrm{ref}}$ under the identity $\PiS
      D_{g,A}^2\PiS^{*}=\mathrm{Lich}$
    of Remark~\ref{rem:q14-symbol}, for which $\EPi$ is the endomorphism of Q14; for a locking operator
    $P\neq P_{\mathrm{ref}}$, $\EPi[P]$ is not the remainder of $D^2_{\Pi,g,A}$ and the identification below is not
    claimed.
    In particular, for $P_{\mathrm{ref}}$ the coefficient $u$ vanishes by Lemma~\ref{lem:chiral-obstruction}, and the
    operator/spectral identification of a non-zero $u$ is not established here.
    The projected square is Laplace-type, $D^2_{\Pi,g,A}=-(\nabla^{S,A})^2+E$, with
    $E=\tfrac{R}{4}+\tfrac12\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu F^a_{\mu\nu}T^a+\EPi$~\cite{Beau2026q14} (with $\EPi$ the endomorphism
    of Q14, that is the compression remainder under the identity of Remark~\ref{rem:q14-symbol}), and the
    $\gamma_5$-weighted Seeley--DeWitt coefficient $a_4=(4\pi)^{-2}\tr(\tfrac12E^2-\tfrac16 RE+\dots)$ in
    the convention of~\cite{Beau2026q14} consistent with the heat-kernel manual.
    The bosonic synthesis fixes the spectral normalisation: $a_2$ is the horizontal gravitational
    response and $a_4$ the vertical gauge response of the spectral entropy functional, with induced
    couplings carrying a common factor at each Seeley--DeWitt order~\cite{Beau2026q13, Beau2026q12}.

    Three facts concern the comparison of the operator and spectral readings of the coefficients of $\EPi^2$.
    First, a coefficient quadratic in $\EPi$ is immune to the linear sign convention of $E$.
    Second, on the gauge-singlet generation sector the gauge curvature term vanishes, and on a flat background all
    curvature cross-terms vanish, in particular $-\tfrac16 R\EPi=0$; only $\tfrac12\EPi^2$ survives, so no
    linear-in-$\EPi$ term can separate the operator and spectral readings.
    Flatness, $R=0$, is a hypothesis of this section, taken in an effective co-metric of Lorentzian signature with
    constant coefficients; the sources leave both the coefficients and the signature of a full-rank co-metric
    conditional~\cite{Beau2026q11}.
    Third, the common $a_4$ normalisation factor cancels in a ratio of generation-reading coefficients such as
    $(\tfrac12+u)/(\tfrac12-u)$.
    Since $u=0$ for the reference residue, nothing is asserted here about a non-zero $u$.

  \section{Chirality is not a finite-fibre label}
    \label{sec:nogo}

    The chirality $\gamma_5$ is the chirality operator of the effective spacetime Clifford
    algebra~\cite{Beau2026q14}, existing under the chiral splitting of Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:chiral-splitting}.
    The supplied spinor carrier has the compact pseudoreal form $\mathfrak{su}(2)$, in which the
    fundamental is self-conjugate (O23 Theorem~3.1, conditional on that
    carrier)~\cite{Beau2026a27}; the Lorentzian complexification is what removes the
    equivalence and renders $\mathcal S_L$ and $\mathcal S_R$ inequivalent~\cite{Beau2026q14}.

    \begin{remark}[No finite chiral edge label]
      \label{rem:nogo}
      A cascade edge $g\to gs$ is a Heisenberg translation, not a symplectic element, so it carries no
      canonical metaplectic lift; and the chiral eigendirections are the complex combinations of the real
      generators, so a real edge has no definite chirality.
      On the admissible front the only orientation-odd coordinate datum reduces to the sign of the
      cocycle, which is the eliminated-amplitude proxy, not a chirality.
      Consequently $u$ cannot be extracted from any finite front observable: the chiral doubling
      $\mathcal S_L\oplus\mathcal S_R$ and the Dirac residue are required.
      This explains, structurally, every vanishing of the symmetric front and shell observables: each
      lacks the chiral asymmetry that lives in the Lorentzian residue.
    \end{remark}

  \section{Finite locking and \texorpdfstring{$J_\Pi$}{JΠ}-equivariance}
    \label{sec:finite-equivariance}

    Before any Lorentzian computation, the finite sector must be excluded as a source of $u$.
    This section records what follows for the finite sector if its locking operator is constructed equivariantly from
      the
    locking data.

    Let $\mathrm{Lock}^{\mathrm{fin}}_S$ denote the projection-locking data of axiom A4~\cite{Beau2026m} at
    finite $q$: the continuous Born--Infeld saturation condition intersected with the discrete shell
    support on $\Heis_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$.
    Write $P_{\mathrm{fin}}$ for the finite locking operator constructed from the locking data; it is in general
      different
    from the reference operator $\PiS^{*}\PiS$, for which $J_\Pi$-commutation is automatic under
      Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:generation}.

    \begin{remark}[Finite equivariance of projection locking]
      \label{prop:finite-equivariance}
      For $q$ an odd prime, the finite locking data are invariant under the parity $c\leftrightarrow q-c$, whose
      identification with $J_\Pi$ is an open problem~\cite{Beau2026a22}: the continuous saturation condition is
      Born--Infeld parity itself, whose spinorial lift is $J_\Pi$~\cite{Beau2026q14}, and the discrete shell support is
      a union of orbits of the parity
      $c\leftrightarrow q-c$~\cite{Beau2026fe}, whose only fixed point on the central character $c\mapsto-c$ is $c=0$,
      so that the support singles out no orientation.
      If $P_{\mathrm{fin}}$ is constructed from the locking data equivariantly, that is with no choice among
      $J_\Pi$-conjugate alternatives, then
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:finite-equiv}
        [\mathrm{Lock}^{\mathrm{fin}}_S,J_\Pi]=0,\qquad [P_{\mathrm{fin}},J_\Pi]=0.
      \end{equation}
      The second relation is the assumption of equivariant construction.
      That the construction involves no choice is motivated by the absence of a finite $J_\Pi$-odd direction: a
      cascade edge $g\to gs$ is a Heisenberg translation, not a symplectic element, so it carries no canonical
      metaplectic lift, and the chiral eigendirections are complex combinations created only by Lorentzian
      complexification~\cite{Beau2026q14}; this is a structural motivation, not a proof.
      No source defines $\EPi[P_{\mathrm{fin}}]$ or $u_{\mathrm{fin}}$ on the finite data.
      If a finite reading of the Schur expression with a reading map as in Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:generation} is supplied,
      Lemma~\ref{lem:chiral-obstruction} gives $u_{\mathrm{fin}}=0$.
    \end{remark}

    The numerical front controls of Appendix~\ref{sec:controls} are consistent with~\eqref{eq:finite-equiv}; they do
    not establish it.
    The symmetric front average vanishes identically, $\Theta_{\mathrm{raw}}=0$ for $q\in\{61,101,151\}$,
    and the oriented frontier is re-paired by the residual automorphism $\varphi=R_b\circ J_\Pi$, the finite
    avatar of the parity.
    These zeros are not failures to detect $u$; they are consistent with finite $J_\Pi$-equivariance.

    \begin{remark}[Finite/Lorentzian separation]
      \label{cor:separation}
      Under the equivariant construction of Remark~\ref{prop:finite-equivariance} and a finite reading as stated there,
      the finite A4 locking does not generate $u$.
      A non-zero generation split then requires a $J_\Pi$-odd datum outside that construction, which this note
      locates in the Lorentzian completion of the Born--Infeld saturation, where the chiral-odd modulus first exists,
      as a hypothesis.
    \end{remark}

    The remaining problem is then the following.
    Let $s$ parametrise the $J_\Pi$-odd deformation of the locked block, with $J_\Pi:s\mapsto -s$; under the
    $J_\Pi$-covariance of the family (as in the companion note~\cite{Beau2026bim}), $u$ is odd in $s$.
    If $J_\Pi$ is a symmetry of the saturation functional $\mathcal B$ of that note, then $\mathcal B(s)=\mathcal
      B(-s)$,
    so $s=0$ is stationary and the local locking character is fixed by the chiral curvature
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:muchi}
      \mu_\chi^2:=\partial_s^2\mathcal B(0).
    \end{equation}
    Interpreting A4 as selection of saturated admissible minima of $\mathcal B$: if $\mu_\chi^2>0$ the symmetric point
      is
    a stable minimum; if $\mu_\chi^2<0$ it is unstable, and a $J_\Pi$-conjugate saturated pair $\pm s_*$ may exist; the
    marginal case $\mu_\chi^2=0$ is decided by the leading non-vanishing even coefficient $\lambda_{2k}$ of
    $\mathcal B(s)=\mathcal B(0)+\lambda_{2k}s^{2k}+O(s^{2k+2})$.
    Whether a symmetry-breaking minimiser carries $u\neq0$ is not shown here: non-commutation with $J_\Pi$ is necessary
    and not sufficient for $u\neq0$ (Remark~\ref{rem:necessary}).
    This marginal case is a degenerate case of the second variation, not an independent source, so the
    decisive step is the necessarily Lorentzian computation of $\mu_\chi^2$, which the A4 companion~\cite{Beau2026bim}
    reduces to the Lorentzian genus of a chiral two-form; the construction of that two-form from the locking data and
    its genus are open there.

    This does not exclude a chiral frontier normalisation after Lorentzian complexification.
    It excludes only a symmetric finite-front observable.
    Once $\gamma_5$ exists, a $J_\Pi$-odd frontier cocycle may supply the discrete factor entering the A4
    matching~\cite{Beau2026q11of}, while the existence and stability of the split remain governed by the
    Lorentzian saturation curvature $\mu_\chi^2$.
    This discrete factor is identified in~\cite{Beau2026cho}: the chiral weight is the Weyl representation type,
    and in the inherited Heisenberg central-phase lift the frontier normalisation is fixed maximally; the split
    $u\neq0$ nonetheless requires a $J_\Pi$-odd datum, whose first-order form is examined below on the same chiral
    object.

  \section{Schur transversality of the A4 locking defect}
    \label{sec:schur-transversality}

    Remark~\ref{rem:necessary} left the A4-level deliverable as a necessary condition that is not yet a
    criterion: the commutator $[P,J_\Pi]\neq0$ may be supported outside the generation triplet, or may die
    under the Weyl transport and the projection to $\Cgen$.
    Section~\ref{sec:finite-equivariance} reduces the modulus to the Lorentzian deformation $s$
    with saturation curvature $\mu_\chi^2$~\eqref{eq:muchi}.
    We now turn the necessary condition into a criterion on the transported,
    generation-projected defect.

    With $s$ the Lorentzian $J_\Pi$-odd deformation parameter of Section~\ref{sec:finite-equivariance}, consider the
    family $\EPi(s):=-\PiS D_{g,A}(1-P(s))D_{g,A}\PiS^{*}$ with $0\preceq P(s)\preceq1$ and $P(0)=\PiS^{*}\PiS$.
    Here $D_{g,A}$ and $\PiS$ are held fixed in the formula, and $P(s)$ is not required to equal $\PiS^{*}\PiS$ for
    $s\neq0$; the first-order motion of the embedding contributes further terms~\cite{Beau2026ebj}.
    Using $\partial_s[-(1-P)]=\partial_sP$, the first-order chiral polarisation is
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:polarisation}
      \mathcal P:=\partial_s\EPi\big|_0=\PiS\,D_{g,A}\,(\partial_sP|_0)\,D_{g,A}\,\PiS^{*}.
    \end{equation}
    The positive sign is the derivative of $-(1-P)$, not a convention.
    Write $T(X):=\PiS D_{g,A}XD_{g,A}\PiS^{*}$ for the Schur transport of an operator $X$ on $\mathcal S$, and
    $T_{\mathrm{out}}(X)$ for the compression of its generation reading $\Phi(T(X))$ to the outer weights
    $\mathrm{span}(e_+,e_-)\subset\mathcal H$ (Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:generation});
    $T$ is the full operator, including its lower-order terms.
    The induced first-order contribution to $\EPi^2$ is the anticommutator $\{\EPi|_0,\mathcal P\}$ with the
    $J_\Pi$-even background; its relation to the split is not examined here.

    \begin{lemma}[Symbol-level transport]
      \label{lem:weyl-faithful}
      Let $\xi$ be a non-null covector.
      Then $\gamma(\xi)^2=g^{-1}(\xi,\xi)\neq0$, so $\gamma(\xi)$ is invertible and the uncompressed symbol map
      $X\mapsto\gamma(\xi)X\gamma(\xi)$ is injective, whether $\xi$ is timelike or spacelike.
      If, however, $(1-P(s))^{1/2}\gamma(\xi)\PiS^{*}=0$ for every $s$ of the family, then
      $\PiS\gamma(\xi)\,(\partial_sP|_0)\,\gamma(\xi)\PiS^{*}=0$: the symbol-level transport of the polarisation
      vanishes, and $T(\partial_sP|_0)$ is a zero-order operator carried by the terms in $\nabla\PiS^{*}$ and
      $B_0\PiS^{*}$.
      The injectivity of the uncompressed symbol map therefore has no bearing on $T_{\mathrm{out}}$.
    \end{lemma}

    \begin{proof}
      $\gamma(\xi)^2=g^{-1}(\xi,\xi)\,\mathbb 1$ by the Clifford relation, which is non-zero for a non-null covector;
      if $\gamma(\xi)X\gamma(\xi)=0$, multiplying on both sides by $\gamma(\xi)^{-1}$ gives $X=0$.
      If~\eqref{eq:hsym} holds for every $s$ then $(1-P(s))\gamma(\xi)\PiS^{*}=0$ for every $s$, because
      $1-P=(1-P)^{1/2}(1-P)^{1/2}$.
      Differentiating at $s=0$ with $\gamma$ and $\PiS$ fixed gives $(\partial_sP|_0)\gamma(\xi)\PiS^{*}=0$, and its
      adjoint $\PiS\gamma(\xi)(\partial_sP|_0)=0$, since $\partial_sP|_0$ is self-adjoint.
      Multiplying gives the claim.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{definition}[Schur transversality]
      \label{def:schur-transversality}
      Assume Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:generation}.
      Let $\sigma:=\tfrac12\big(\partial_sP|_0-J_\Pi(\partial_sP|_0)J_\Pi^{-1}\big)$ be the $J_\Pi$-odd part of the
      opened direction, a linear operator on $\mathcal S$, and
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:transversality}
        T_{\mathrm{out}}(\sigma):=\Pi_{\mathrm{out}}\,\Phi\big(\PiS\,D_{g,A}\,\sigma\,D_{g,A}\,\PiS^{*}\big)\,
        \Pi_{\mathrm{out}},
      \end{equation}
      where $\Pi_{\mathrm{out}}$ is the orthogonal projection of $\mathcal H$ onto $\mathrm{span}(e_+,e_-)$.
      The A4 locking defect is \emph{Schur-transverse} if $T_{\mathrm{out}}(\sigma)\neq0$ and \emph{Schur-null}
      otherwise.
    \end{definition}

    \begin{proposition}[Schur-null and Schur-transverse loci]
      \label{prop:schur-dichotomy}
      Under Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:generation}, $T_{\mathrm{out}}(\sigma)$ is the $J$-odd part, compressed to the outer
      weights, of the generation reading $\Phi(\mathcal P)$ of the first-order polarisation $\mathcal P$
      of~\eqref{eq:polarisation}, where $J$ is the antiunitary of $\mathcal H$.
      Hence, for the family $\EPi(s)$, this $J$-odd part vanishes in the Schur-null case and is non-zero in the
      Schur-transverse case.
      The first-order change of $\EPi^2$ is the anticommutator $\{\EPi|_0,\mathcal P\}$, which involves entries of
      $\mathcal P$ outside the outer weights; no statement is made here about the opening of the split at first order.
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}
      The transport $T(X)=\PiS D_{g,A}XD_{g,A}\PiS^{*}$ satisfies $J_\Pi T(X)J_\Pi^{-1}=T(J_\Pi XJ_\Pi^{-1})$ by
      Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:generation}(i), and $J\Phi(Y)J^{-1}=\Phi(J_\Pi YJ_\Pi^{-1})$ by~(iii).
      Hence $\Phi(T(\sigma))$ is the $J$-odd part of $\Phi(T(\partial_sP|_0))=\Phi(\mathcal P)$.
      The compression to $\mathrm{span}(e_+,e_-)$ commutes with taking the $J$-odd part because $J$ exchanges $e_+$ and
      $e_-$ and preserves $\mathrm{span}(e_+,e_-)$.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{lemma}[Exact metaplectic opening of the Schur defect]
      \label{lem:exact-schur-opening}
      Let $V\simeq\mathbb C^2$ carry the standard $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-triple
      \[
        E=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
        F=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
        H=[E,F],
      \]
      with $H=2J_3$ on the outer generation weights of $\operatorname{Sym}^2(V)=\langle e_0,e_+,e_-\rangle$, and let
      $g(x,y)=\exp(xE)\exp(yF)$, with real shear parameters $x,y$ (distinct from the trajectory modulus $s$), be the oriented metaplectic step of the cascade bridge~\cite{Beau2026q14}.
      Then
      \[
        g(x,y)=\begin{pmatrix}1+xy&x\\ y&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad\det g(x,y)=1,
      \]
      and, for $xy>0$, with $\cosh r=1+\tfrac{xy}{2}$, $r>0$ real,
      \[
        \log g(x,y)=\frac{r}{\sinh r}\left(xE+yF+\frac{xy}{2}H\right).
      \]
      Consequently the $J_\Pi$-odd Cartan component of the generator $\log g(x,y)$ on the generation triplet, produced
      by the $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ commutator $[E,F]=H$ (Q14, Proposition~6.5; the Heisenberg bracket $[X,Y]=Z$ does not
      appear in this computation), is
      \[
        \alpha(x,y)=xy\,\frac{r}{\sinh r}=xy+O\!\left((xy)^2\right),
      \]
      while the $R_{\mathrm{mix}}$ component vanishes identically, $\mu(x,y)\equiv0$.
      In particular $\alpha(x,y)\neq0$ for every oriented step with $xy>0$.
    \end{lemma}

    \begin{proof}
      The matrix form follows from $E^2=F^2=0$, giving
      $\exp(xE)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&x\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ and
      $\exp(yF)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\y&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.
      The element $g(x,y)$ lies in $\mathrm{SL}_2$ with $\operatorname{tr}g(x,y)=2+xy$, hence
      $\cosh r=\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}g(x,y)=1+\tfrac{xy}{2}$.
      For a hyperbolic $\mathrm{SL}_2$ element the logarithm is
      $\log g=\frac{r}{\sinh r}\big(g-\tfrac12(\operatorname{tr}g)\,\mathbb 1\big)$, and here
      $g(x,y)-\tfrac12(\operatorname{tr}g)\,\mathbb 1=xE+yF+\tfrac{xy}{2}H$.
      Since $H=2J_3$, the $J_3$ coefficient is $xy\,(r/\sinh r)$, and $r/\sinh r=1+O(xy)$ gives
      $\alpha(x,y)=xy+O((xy)^2)$.
      Lifting to $\operatorname{Sym}^2(V)$, the horizontal generators $E,F$ send $e_0$ to $e_\pm$ and $e_\pm$ to $e_0$ or $0$
      (never to the other outer weight), so their restriction to $\langle e_+,e_-\rangle$ carries no $R_{\mathrm{mix}}$
      component; only the Cartan $J_3$ survives, whence $\mu(x,y)\equiv0$, the mixing direction being absent in the
      real symplectic model~\cite{Beau2026q14}.
      The factor $r/\sinh r$ is strictly positive for $r>0$, so $\alpha(x,y)\neq0$ for $xy>0$.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{hypothesis}[Transport of the opening to the locking operator, {[H-Tr]}]
      \label{hyp:transport}
      Let $P(s;x,y)$ be a family of locking operators along the $J_\Pi$-odd modulus $s$ of
      Section~\ref{sec:finite-equivariance}, indexed by the oriented steps $(x,y)$ with
      $xy>0$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:exact-schur-opening}.
      There is a real $\kappa\neq0$, independent of the step, such that
      $T_{\mathrm{out}}(\sigma_{(x,y)})
      =\kappa\,\alpha(x,y)\,J_3\big|_{\mathrm{span}(e_+,e_-)}$
      for every such step.
    \end{hypothesis}

    No source relates $P(s)$ to the metaplectic step $g(x,y)$: Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:transport} is a missing element,
    not an excluded one.
    Under the symbol condition along the family it can hold only on a fibre with a multiplicity space.
    Indeed, on an irreducible fibre Proposition~\ref{prop:irreducible} gives $P(0)=P_{\mathrm{ref}}=1$; since
    $1-P(s)\succeq0$ vanishes at $s=0$, its derivative vanishes there, $\partial_sP|_0=0$, hence $\sigma=0$ and
    $T_{\mathrm{out}}(\sigma)=0$, contradicting $\kappa\alpha\neq0$.
    Under the symbol condition along the family, the symbol-level part of $T$ vanishes
    (Lemma~\ref{lem:weyl-faithful}), so a non-zero $T_{\mathrm{out}}(\sigma)$ is carried by the zero-order terms in
    $\nabla\PiS^{*}$ and $B_0\PiS^{*}$; this is part of what Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:transport} asserts and is examined
    nowhere in the corpus.
    No identification of the central commutator $Z=[X,Y]$ with a temporal direction is used, and none is available:
    the central entry of the co-metric of Q5b~\cite{Beau2026q5b} (Theorem~5.2) is zero at rank three, it is positive in
    the conditional full-rank completion of Q8~\cite{BeauQ8} (Corollary~6.1), and it is negative in
    neither~\cite{Beau2026q11}.

    \begin{corollary}[Schur transversality under the transport hypothesis]
      \label{cor:schur-transversality-closed}
      Assume the compatibility of Definition~\ref{def:schur-transversality} and Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:transport}.
      Then the A4 locking defect is Schur-transverse for every oriented step with $xy>0$.
    \end{corollary}

    \begin{proof}
      $T_{\mathrm{out}}(\sigma)=\kappa\alpha J_3|_{\mathrm{span}(e_+,e_-)}$ with $\kappa\neq0$, $\alpha\neq0$ by
      Lemma~\ref{lem:exact-schur-opening}, and $J_3=\mathrm{diag}(1,-1)$ on $\mathrm{span}(e_+,e_-)$.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{remark}[A4-level reduction]
      \label{cor:a4-reduction}
      The A4 residual problem is reduced to the projected Lorentzian datum $T_{\mathrm{out}}(\sigma)$ of
      Definition~\ref{def:schur-transversality}.
      If the locking is Schur-transverse, the local behaviour of the saturation margin at the symmetric point is set by
      the Born--Infeld genus of the chiral two-form supplied in~\cite{Beau2026bim}; the consequences for $u$ are
      stated there under that note's own hypotheses.
      If the locking is Schur-null, the first-order polarisation is absent; in the reduction of~\cite{Beau2026bim}, for
      the supplied two-form, $\mu_\chi^2=0$ and the split is governed by the leading even coefficient $\lambda_{2k}$
      of~\eqref{eq:muchi}.
    \end{remark}

    Proposition~\ref{prop:schur-dichotomy} identifies the transported $J_\Pi$-odd tilt $T_{\mathrm{out}}(\sigma)$ as the
    $J_\Pi$-odd first-order polarisation on the outer weights; which of the two loci holds is open.
    Lemma~\ref{lem:exact-schur-opening} evaluates the $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ commutator of the oriented step in closed form;
    under Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:transport} the locking is Schur-transverse.
    That hypothesis is not supplied by a source, and the symbol-level transport vanishes
    (Lemma~\ref{lem:weyl-faithful}), so that it concerns zero-order terms.
    The Lorentzian datum $\partial_sP|_0$ is not constructed: its relation to the metaplectic step
    $g(x,y)=\exp(xE)\exp(yF)$ is Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:transport}.
    The sign of the Born--Infeld genus of~\cite{Beau2026bim} is not decided here.

  \section{Status of results}
    \label{sec:status}

    \noindent\emph{Proved / structural (this note).}
    \begin{itemize}
      \item For every locking operator $P$, $\EPi[P]=-\PiS D(1-P)D\PiS^{*}=-N_P^{\dagger}N_P\preceq0$
      (Proposition~\ref{prop:schur}); for $P=P_{\mathrm{ref}}=\PiS^{*}\PiS$ under Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:contraction} it is
      the compression remainder of the projected Dirac square.
      It is zero-order under Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:symbol} (Lemma~\ref{lem:order}).
      For $P_{\mathrm{ref}}$ on irreducible Clifford fibres that condition forces $\EPi=0$
      (Proposition~\ref{prop:irreducible}).
      $P_{\mathrm{ref}}^2=P_{\mathrm{ref}}$ holds if and only if $\PiS$ is a partial isometry
      (Remark~\ref{rem:idempotence}).
      \item Chiral diagonal blocks~\eqref{eq:diag}; $\pi_{LL}=0$ implies left-admissibility, and the converse holds
      under Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:faithful} (Proposition~\ref{prop:chiralblocks}).
      \item The $J_3$-odd component of $\EPi^2$ is read, as a structural reading, in the chiral block decomposition as
      a raw asymmetry $\pi_{RR}-\pi_{LL}$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:localise}), and refined in
      Section~\ref{sec:spinorial-locking} as the antiunitary equivariance defect
      $\Delta_\chi(P)=\pi_{LL}-\tau\,\overline{\pi_{RR}}\,\tau^{-1}$, after $\Deff^{\pm}$-transport and
      projection to $\Cgen$.
      \item $J_\Pi PJ_\Pi^{-1}=P\Rightarrow u=0$ under Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:generation}; on the interpretive reading of
      axioms
      A1--A4, a
      non-zero $u$ requires a breaking of $J_\Pi$-equivariance by A4 (Proposition~\ref{prop:strat}).
      \item The symmetric-square action of the Born--Infeld involution is fixed: $J_\Pi^2=-1$ on the
      fundamental doublet, $(J_\Pi^{(2)})^2=+1$ on the generation triplet, and it anticommutes with
      $J_3$~\eqref{eq:jpij3}.
      Hence $u\neq0$ requires a failure of $J_\Pi$-equivariance at projection locking
      (Lemma~\ref{lem:chiral-obstruction}); the magnitude of $u$ depends on the $\Deff^{\pm}$-transported,
      generation-projected component of the antiunitary defect $\Delta_\chi(P)$~\eqref{eq:defect}.
      \item Section~\ref{sec:conventions} fixes the Seeley--DeWitt conventions for the reference residue on a flat
      background, where $u=0$; no spectral definition of $u$ is given for a general locking operator.
      \item The exact $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ opening $\alpha(x,y)=xy\,(r/\sinh r)\not\equiv0$ of the oriented step, produced
      by $[E,F]=H$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:exact-schur-opening}); under $J_\Pi$-compatibility, the transported tilt
      $T_{\mathrm{out}}(\sigma)$ is the $J_\Pi$-odd first-order polarisation on the outer weights
      (Proposition~\ref{prop:schur-dichotomy}).
    \end{itemize}

    \noindent\emph{Conditional.}
    Chirality is argued to be a Lorentzian rather than a finite-fibre datum, and $u$ not a front observable
    (Remark~\ref{rem:nogo}).
    The finite locking data are invariant under the parity $c\leftrightarrow q-c$, whose identification with $J_\Pi$ is
    open; under that identification, an equivariant construction of the finite locking operator and a finite reading,
    $u_{\mathrm{fin}}=0$ (Remarks~\ref{prop:finite-equivariance} and~\ref{cor:separation}).
    The left-admissible branch of~\cite{Beau2026q14} is not reachable, under Hypotheses~\ref{hyp:chiral-splitting}
    and~\ref{hyp:generation}(i), by a $J_\Pi$-commuting locking operator with a non-zero residue (Remark~\ref{rem:q14-left}); its derivation from A4 is open.
    The Schur-transverse locus (Corollary~\ref{cor:schur-transversality-closed}) holds under the
    $J_\Pi$-compatibility of Definition~\ref{def:schur-transversality} and the transport
    hypothesis~\ref{hyp:transport}.
    The reduction of $u$ to the Born--Infeld genus of~\cite{Beau2026bim} holds for a chiral two-form supplied there.

    \noindent\emph{Open.}
    Which locus holds is open, as are the transport hypothesis~\ref{hyp:transport}, the explicit

    The magnitude $|u|$ depends on the fully transported and projected Born--Infeld saturation data,
    including the chiral-frontier normalisation, and is not determined by the Schur-transversality argument
    itself.

  \section{Open deliverables}
    \label{sec:open}

    The finite contribution to A4 is separated only conditionally: under an equivariant construction and a finite
    reading, finite projection locking does not generate $u$
    (Remarks~\ref{prop:finite-equivariance} and~\ref{cor:separation}).
    The Schur-transversality test that conditions the A4 companion is the non-vanishing of the transported tilt
    $T_{\mathrm{out}}(\sigma)$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:schur-dichotomy}); it holds under
    Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:transport} (Corollary~\ref{cor:schur-transversality-closed}).
    The Born--Infeld genus of~\cite{Beau2026bim} then decides, for the chiral two-form supplied there, between
    $u=0$ and $u\neq0$.

    The open deliverables are:
    \begin{enumerate}
      \item the relation between the locking operator $P(s)$ and the metaplectic step $g(x,y)$
      (Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:transport});
      \item the explicit Lorentzian eliminated block $1-P(s)$ entering the Schur complement
      \[
        \EPi=-\PiS\,D_{g,A}\,(1-P(s))\,D_{g,A}\,\PiS^{*};
      \]
      \item the absolute normalisation $|u|$, including the fully transported Born--Infeld saturation data, the
      cubic Schur backreaction, and the chiral-frontier normalisation;
      \item the projected Yukawa sector and the corresponding mass matrices.
    \end{enumerate}
    The structural form of the first deliverable is constrained in the companion note~\cite{Beau2026ebj}: for every
    smooth family the eliminated-block jet $1-P(s) = Q_0 + sQ_1 + s^2Q_2 + O(s^3)$ has $Q_1 = [A, Q_0]$ a purely
    off-diagonal Grassmann tangent, and, for the antiunitary parity written in the chiral frame as
    $\Sigma_\tau \circ \mathrm{conj}$ with $\Sigma_\tau = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} 0 & \tau \\ \tau^{-1} & 0
    \end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, $\tau$ the intertwiner of Section~\ref{sec:spinorial-locking}, the left-handed diagonal
    block of its $J_\Pi$-odd part is half the transported equivariance defect,
    \[
      [\Pi_{J_\Pi\text{-odd}}(1-P)]_{LL} = \tfrac12\Delta_\chi(P),
      \qquad
      [\Pi_{J_\Pi\text{-odd}}\dot Q(0)]_{LL} = \tfrac12\,\partial_s\Delta_\chi(P)\big|_0,
      \qquad \dot Q(0) = Q_1.
    \]
    This determines one block of the odd tangent, not a unique generator, which is defined only modulo the centraliser
    of $Q_0$; the same note exhibits a covariant family with a moving spinorial embedding realising the even normal form
    $\mathrm{diag}(1, \tfrac12, \tfrac12)$ at the base point with a non-zero first-order split rate.
    The admissible normalisation of $\partial_s\Delta_\chi(P)|_0$ is therefore one component of the second deliverable,
    the selection of the family and the injective localisation of the $J_\Pi$-odd tangent onto its left-handed block
    remaining open, with $|u|$ dictionary-bound.
    These are the remaining conceptual steps before the explicit construction of $\EPi$ as a Lorentzian
    $J_\Pi$-odd projected Dirac residue.

  \section{Conclusion}
    \label{sec:conclusion}

    A non-zero generation split requires, under Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:generation}, a locking operator with
    $J_\Pi PJ_\Pi^{-1}\neq P$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:chiral-obstruction}).
    The antiunitary chiral equivariance defect
    \[
      \Delta_\chi(P)=\pi_{LL}-\tau\,\overline{\pi_{RR}}\,\tau^{-1}
    \]
    is built from the blocks of the eliminated operator $1-P$ entering the Schur complement
    $\EPi=-N^{\dagger}N$; whether the magnitude of $u$ is carried by its transported, generation-projected component
    is a structural reading.
    It vanishes for the $J_\Pi$-equivariant minimal non-injectivity on the interpretive reading of the axioms
    (Proposition~\ref{prop:strat}).
    The polarisation and the Seeley--DeWitt conventions are fixed in the conditional sense of the corresponding
    sections; the front observables are null controls rather than carriers of $u$.
    Under Hypotheses~\ref{hyp:chiral-splitting} and~\ref{hyp:generation}(i), a non-zero residue of a $J_\Pi$-commuting
    locking operator cannot lie on the left-admissible branch of Q14 (Remark~\ref{rem:q14-left}); a general $P$ is
    not covered.
    The note localises necessary conditions and missing constructions; it does not derive a physical mechanism for a
    non-zero split.
    The transported $J_\Pi$-odd tilt of the locking operator is the first-order polarisation on the outer weights
    (Proposition~\ref{prop:schur-dichotomy}); which of the Schur-null and Schur-transverse loci holds is open.
    The exact $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ opening of the oriented step (Lemma~\ref{lem:exact-schur-opening}) gives
    the Schur-transverse locus under the transport hypothesis~\ref{hyp:transport}.
    Whether $u\neq0$ then follows depends on the Born--Infeld genus of the A4 companion~\cite{Beau2026bim}, which is
    stated there for a chiral two-form supplied as a hypothesis.
    This note does not decide it, and does not determine the value $|u|$, which remains fixed by the chiral-frontier
    normalisation through the Born--Infeld saturation structure~\cite{Beau2026c}.
